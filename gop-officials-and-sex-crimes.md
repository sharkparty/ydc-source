# Republican Officials and Sex Crimes

A list of Republican officeholders, candidates, party officials, and movement
activists convicted of — or charged with — sex offenses, most of them against
children.

---

## Scope and limitations

**Provenance.** A version of this list has circulated online for close to two
decades, passed between forums and chain emails, and entered this repository in
that form. Lists of that kind accumulate errors. Each entry below has been
checked against its source; where the source is thin or absent, the entry says
so. Two entries could not be traced to any source other than further copies of
the same list and are marked **Unverified**.

**What this does and does not show.** This is not a base-rate claim. It has no
denominator, no matched comparison, and no sampling method, and therefore cannot
establish that Republican officials commit these offenses at a higher rate than
any other group. It does not support that argument.

What it does establish is narrower: these specific individuals, many of whom
built careers on protecting children from asserted threats — gay adoptive
parents, drag performers, sex education, pornography — committed offenses against
children. The divergence between stated platform and documented conduct requires
no statistical claim.

**The entries.** *Charged* and *convicted* are distinct, both appear below, and
each entry specifies which. Several cases are decades old, and some links resolve
to archived or defunct pages, retained so the citation remains traceable.

## Officeholders and candidates

**Dennis Hastert** — Speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives, 1999–2007,
and at the time the longest-serving Republican Speaker in history. Indicted on
federal charges of structuring bank withdrawals after prosecutors alleged he had
molested at least four boys, some as young as 14, while working as a high school
wrestling coach, and had paid to conceal it. He admitted the abuse. Sentenced to
15 months.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/07/18/us/dennis-hastert-released.html))

**Ralph Shortey** — Oklahoma state senator and chair of Trump's 2016 Oklahoma
campaign. Indicted on four counts of human trafficking and child pornography; in
November 2017 pleaded guilty to one count of child sex trafficking in exchange
for dismissal of the others. Sentenced to 15 years.
([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/feature/nbc-out/trump-s-oklahoma-campaign-chair-plead-guilt-child-sex-trafficking-n822461?fbclid=IwAR142W77Q5Dan71BsxC_5uH8h1BBA4EGyqP_VMsrx7lSvoPX9Njjvt0oHK0),
[The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/post-nation/wp/2017/11/20/former-oklahoma-state-senator-admits-to-child-sex-trafficking-while-in-office/))

*(This list originally carried Shortey twice — once as Trump's campaign chair and
once as a state senator. Same man, same case; merged here.)*

**Tim Nolan** — former judge and chair of Trump's 2016 Kentucky campaign.
Pleaded guilty to child sex trafficking; sentenced February 11, 2018 to 20 years
in prison.
([Cincinnati Enquirer](https://www.cincinnati.com/story/news/2018/05/03/former-judge-tim-nolan-could-sentenced-today-more-drama-could-get-way/577947002/))

**Jim Knoblach** — Minnesota state representative. Dropped out of his 2018
re-election race in September 2018 after his daughter said publicly that he had
molested her for more than ten years, beginning when she was 12.
([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2018/09/22/lawmaker-quits-race-after-daughter-says-he-molested-her-more-than-decade/?utm_term=.8ac8527c7f43))

**Donald "Buz" Lukens** — U.S. Congressman from Ohio. Convicted of having sex
with a minor; sentenced to one month in jail.
([The New York Times, 1989](https://www.nytimes.com/1989/05/25/us/teen-ager-in-ohio-testifies-to-sex-with-a-congressman.html))

**Dan Crane** — U.S. Congressman from Illinois. Censured by the House in 1983

([Dan Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Crane))

**Robert Bauman** — U.S. Congressman from Maryland and a prominent anti-gay

**Philip Giordano** — Mayor of Waterbury, Connecticut. Serving a 37-year federal

([Philip Giordano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Giordano))

**Mark Pazuhanich** — Pennsylvania judge. Pleaded no contest to fondling a

([Pocono Record](http://www.poconorecord.com/article/20120426/NEWS90/204260334))

**Peter Dibble** — Connecticut legislator and first selectman. Pleaded no

([The New York Times, 2004](https://www.nytimes.com/2004/04/18/nyregion/embroiled-first-selectman-takes-leave.html))

**David Swartz** — county commissioner. Pleaded guilty to molesting two girls

**Strom Thurmond** — U.S. Senator, segregationist, and the author of the longest

([Strom Thurmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strom_Thurmond))

## Activists, clergy, and media figures

**Earl "Butch" Kimmerling** — anti-gay activist who, with his wife, fought in
1998 to stop a gay man from adopting an 8-year-old foster girl — the sister of
three boys the same man had already adopted. The Kimmerlings won custody and
adopted her in October 1998. He pleaded guilty the following January to four
counts of molesting her and was sentenced to 40 years. In 2019, before the
Indiana Parole Board, he admitted to molesting four additional children; his
parole was revoked.
([Herald Bulletin](https://www.heraldbulletin.com/news/new-molestation-charge-filed-against-kimmerling/article_00011fca-0b23-11ea-b8b2-0727ae0426f5.html),

[contemporaneous sentencing coverage](https://poundpuplegacy.org/document/130159))

**Mike Hintz** — youth pastor at First Assembly of God in Des Moines, singled

had benefited from his tax plan. Days later Hintz surrendered to police,
admitting a sexual relationship with a 17-year-old girl from his youth group
whom he had been counseling for emotional problems; contact occurred at her home
and during an overseas mission trip. He was fired on October 30, 2004, pleaded
guilty to sexual exploitation by a counselor, and was placed on the Iowa sex
offender registry.
([Quad-City Times / qconline](https://qconline.com/news/iowa/article_c1094db3-19f0-55b5-aae4-55ba62823884.html),
