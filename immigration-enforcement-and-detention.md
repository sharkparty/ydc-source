# Immigration Enforcement and Detention

Second term, 2025–2026. The detention build-out, the proposal to deputize a
private force, and the people killed by federal agents on American streets.

## 1. The detention expansion

The facility that became the symbol of it is the Everglades site the state
branded "Alligator Alcatraz."

**How it was built.** Governor Ron DeSantis used a 2023 emergency executive
order — repeatedly extended since — to seize the land, hire contractors, and
suspend "any statute, rule or order" that stood in the way. County officials
learned about it from the news. Emails obtained under public records law
document the process.
([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/gov-desantis-blindsided-florida-county-officials-with-alligator-alcatraz-plans-emails-show#:~:text=relying%20on%20an%20executive%20order%20to%20seize%20the%20land,%20hire%20contractors%20and%20bypass%20laws%20and%20regulations))

**The expansion.** On August 14, 2025, the state announced a second site: Baker
Correctional Institution in Sanderson, redesignated a "Deportation Depot" to
detain and process people for removal.
([Florida Governor's Office press release](https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2025/governor-ron-desantis-announces-expansion-floridas-capacity-detain-and-deport#:~:text=Baker%20Correctional%20Institution%20in%20Sanderson%20will%20now%20serve%20as%20a%20Deportation%20Depot%20to%20detain%20and%20process%20illegal%20immigrants%20for%20removal))

**The conditions.** Congressional visitors reported cages holding 30 or more
people at temperatures near 100°F. Detainees described minimal and sometimes
contaminated food, insect infestations, overflowing toilets, and no medical
care — conditions severe enough to produce hunger strikes.
([ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/news/immigrants-rights/floridas-secretive-immigration-detention-center-explained#:~:text=Congressional%20visitors%20reported%20cages%20holding%2030%20or%20more%20individuals%20at%20temperatures%20near%20100%20degrees.%20Detainees%20report%20minimal%20and%20sometimes%20contaminated%20food,%20bug%20infestations,%20overflowing%20toilets,%20and%20lack%20of%20medical%20care%20%E2%80%94%20conditions%20so%20severe%20that%20they%20have%20led%20the%20detainees%20to%20stage%20a%20hunger%20s))

**The litigation.** *C.M. v. Noem*, filed in the Southern District of Florida on
July 16, 2025 by the ACLU and Americans for Immigrant Justice on behalf of
detainees, over conditions and denial of access to counsel.

**The human rights finding.** Amnesty International's December 2025 report,
*Torture and Enforced Disappearances in the Sunshine State*, documents
conditions at Alligator Alcatraz and at Krome, and calls on Florida to close the
Everglades facility and to prohibit any state-run immigration detention.
([Amnesty International USA, PDF](https://www.amnestyusa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Torture-and-Enforced-Disappearances-in-the-Sunshine-State-Human-Rights-Violations-at-Alligator-Alcatraz-and-Krome-in-Florida.pdf#:~:text=calls%20on%20the%20Government%20of.%20Florida%20to%20close%20%E2%80%9CAlligator%20Alcatraz%E2%80%9D,%20to%20prohibit%20the%20use%20of%20any%20state-run%20immigration%20detention%20facility))

**On the word "concentration camp":** it is a characterization used by critics
and by some historians of the term. It is not a legal finding, and the primary
documents above describe immigration detention facilities. The documented facts —
emergency powers used to bypass law, 100°F cages, denial of counsel, an Amnesty
finding of torture and enforced disappearance — do not need the label to be
damning, and the argument is stronger without it.

## 2. The private-army proposal

**No private military was created.** What is documented is a proposal that
reached the White House and got a hearing there.

**The memo.** A 26-page document obtained by Politico in February 2025, from
Erik Prince and the group 2USV, proposed deputizing 10,000 private citizens —
veterans, former law enforcement, retired ICE and CBP officers — giving them
expedited training and the same federal law enforcement powers as immigration
officers.
([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/02/25/documents-military-contractors-mass-deportations-022648#:~:text=suggest%20deputizing%2010,000%20private%20citizens,%20including%20military%20veterans,%20former%20law%20enforcement%20officials%20and%20retired%20ICE%20and%20CBP%20officers,%20giving%20them%20expedited%20training%20and%20the%20same%20federal%20law%20enforcement%20powers%20of%20immigration%20officials))

The full package: a network of "processing camps" on military bases, a private
fleet of 100 aircraft, and a "small army" of private citizens empowered to make
arrests — roughly $25 billion, to deport 12 million people.

**The reception.** White House officials held multiple conversations with the
contractors, and Trump appeared open to using private forces for deportations.
([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/02/27/trump-private-forces-immigration-00206560#:~:text=a%20network%20of%20%E2%80%9Cprocessing%20camps%E2%80%9D%20on%20military%20bases,%20a%20private%20fleet%20of%20100%20planes,%20and%20a%20%E2%80%9Csmall%20army%E2%80%9D%20of%20private%20citizens%20empowered%20to%20make%20arrests.%20The%2026-page%20document%20carries%20an%20estimated%20price%20tag%20of%20$25%20billion%20and%20recommends%20a%20range%20of%20aggressive%20tactics%20to%20rapidly%20deport%2012%20million%20people%20befo))

**What was used instead — and it is worse.** Rather than a private force
operating in a legal grey zone, the administration federalized National Guard
units and deployed them domestically. Two federal judges found the deployments
partly illegal under the Posse Comitatus Act: Judge Charles Breyer (N.D. Cal.,
September 2, 2025) and Judge Jia Cobb (D.D.C., November 20, 2025). Trump has
separately spoken publicly about invoking the Insurrection Act to turn the
military into a domestic police force.
([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/03/nx-s1-5593112/national-guard-mass-deportations-trump-2026#:~:text=the%20invocation%20of%20the%20Insurrection%20Act%20to%20deputize%20the%20military%20to%20enforce%20domestic%20law%20to%20basically%20become%20a%20domestic%20police%20force))

The Prince memo matters not because it was adopted but because it establishes
what was on the table, and that nobody in the room treated it as unthinkable.

## 3. Americans killed by federal agents

**Operation Metro Surge** ran from December 4, 2025 to February 12, 2026, in the
Minneapolis–Saint Paul area, run by DHS, CBP, Border Patrol, and ICE. It
produced more than 4,000 arrests and at least nine deaths.

Two of the dead were U.S. citizens, killed on camera while observing immigration
enforcement — activity protected by the First Amendment.

**Renée Nicole Good** — 37, a mother of three. Shot three times by a federal
immigration agent during a January 7, 2026 traffic stop, including a fatal shot
to the head. The Hennepin County Medical Examiner ruled the death a **homicide**.

**Alex Pretti** — 37, an intensive care nurse. Shot and killed by federal agents
at a January 24, 2026 protest.

**The government's account is disputed by video.** CBP submitted a report to
Congress on January 27, 2026. Bystander footage reviewed by the AP, Reuters, and
the BBC shows an agent removing Pretti's gun before he was shot.

**No one has been charged.** As of July 2026, no federal charges had been filed
in connection with any of the nine deaths, and the federal government has argued
that state prosecutors lack jurisdiction over federal officers.
([ABC7 New York](https://abc7ny.com/post/dhs-ice-shootings-minnesota-prosecutors-obtain-long-withheld-evidence-renee-good-alex-pretti-shooting-deaths/19501175/#:~:text=At%20least%20nine%20people%20have%20been%20killed%20since%20the%20Trump%20administration's%20immigration%20enforcement%20campaign%20began%20last%20year.%20No%20one%20has%20been%20charged%20in%20connection%20with%20the%20deaths,%20and%20the%20federal%20government%20has%20suggested%20state%20prosecutors%20don't%20have%20jurisdiction%20to%20investigate%20federal%20officers))

**The evidence was withheld for months.** Minnesota sued the Justice Department
and DHS on March 24, 2026, alleging that evidence was being withheld to shield
the officers. Bodycam footage and Good's SUV were finally turned over on July
13, 2026.
([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/13/feds-have-turned-over-evidence-in-good-pretti-sosacelis-shootings),
[Minnesota Reformer](https://minnesotareformer.com/2026/07/13/feds-turns-over-evidence-in-killings-of-renee-good-alex-pretti-by-federal-immigration-agents/),
[PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/minnesota-prosecutors-obtain-long-withheld-evidence-in-probe-into-fatal-shootings-of-good-pretti))

**Trump's comment**, to NBC on February 4, 2026: the killings "should have not
happened."
([6ABC](https://6abc.com/live-updates/minneapolis-ice-shooting-live-updates-county-medical-examiner-lists-alex-prettis-death-homicide/18528885/#:~:text=Renee%20Good,%20Alex%20Pretti%20killings%20'should%20have%20not%20happened))

**On the phrase "killing US citizens indiscriminately":** the deaths are
documented and a medical examiner called one of them homicide. "Indiscriminate"
overstates what the record shows — these were specific shootings in specific
encounters, and each is being contested on its own facts. The pattern that the
record does support is narrower and still serious: lethal force used on citizens
observing enforcement, an official account contradicted by video, evidence
withheld for six months, and no charges.
