# Pardons and Clemency

The pardon power is the least reviewable authority a president has. What a
president does with it is therefore unusually revealing.

## February 18, 2020: seven pardons, four commutations

Granted the day the Senate's acquittal was three weeks old.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/02/18/us/politics/trump-pardons.html))

### The public-corruption cases

- **Rod R. Blagojevich** — sentenced to 14 years in 2011 for attempting to sell
  or trade the U.S. Senate seat vacated by Barack Obama.
  ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2011/12/08/us/blagojevich-expresses-remorse-in-courtroom-speech.html))
  *Commuted.*

- **Bernard B. Kerik** — the former NYPD commissioner, sentenced to four years
  after pleading guilty to eight felonies including tax fraud and lying to White
  House officials during his own vetting for Secretary of Homeland Security.
  ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2010/02/19/nyregion/19kerik.html))
  *Pardoned.*

- **David Safavian** — chief of staff of the General Services Administration,
  sentenced to a year in prison in 2009 for obstruction of justice and false
  statements covering up his ties to lobbyist Jack Abramoff.
  ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/former-gsa-chief-staff-david-safavian-sentenced-obstruction-justice-and-making-false))
  *Pardoned.*

- **Edward DeBartolo Jr.** — former San Francisco 49ers owner, pleaded guilty in
  1998 to concealing an extortion plot by a former governor of Louisiana.
  ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/02/18/us/politics/trump-pardon-blagojevich-debartolo.html))
  *Pardoned.*

### The white-collar cases

- **Michael R. Milken** — the junk bond financier, pleaded guilty to securities
  fraud and conspiracy, sentenced to 10 years, reduced to two.
  ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/02/18/us/politics/trump-pardons.html))
  *Pardoned.*

- **Judith Negron** — sentenced to 35 years in 2011 for her role in a $205
  million Medicare fraud scheme as owner of a Miami mental health company.
  ([FBI](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/miami/press-releases/2011/owner-of-miami-area-mental-health-company-sentenced-to-35-years-in-prison-for-orchestrating-205-million-medicare-fraud-scheme-1))

- **Paul Pogue** — sentenced to three years' probation and ordered to pay
  $723,000 in fines and restitution for filing false income tax statements.
  ([Star Local Media](https://starlocalmedia.com/mckinneycouriergazette/news/pogue-gets-probation-in-tax-fraud-trial/article_cb54abd1-4ef6-5e92-bd28-af1709118bad.html))
  *Pardoned.*

  **Context:** The Pogue family donated over $200,000 to Trump's re-election
  effort in the months before the pardon.

- **Ariel Friedler** — pleaded guilty in 2014 to conspiracy to access a

### The drug and property cases

These three are the ones most defensible on the merits, and they are worth
separating out rather than lumping in:

- **Tynice Nichole Hall** — sentenced in 2006 on drug charges in Lubbock, Texas.
  The evidence at trial showed her residence was used as a stash house by her
  boyfriend, the actual target of the investigation.
  ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/archive/usao/txn/PressRel06/hall_tynice_etal_drug_lubbock_sen_pr.html))
  *Commuted.*

- **Crystal Munoz** — found guilty in 2008 of conspiring to possess marijuana
  with intent to distribute; served over a decade.
  ([CBS7](https://www.cbs7.com/content/news/West-Texas-woman-submits-petition-to-president-for-sentence-reduction-510795721.html))
  *Commuted.*

- **Angela Stanton** — served six months of home confinement in 2007 for her
  role in a stolen-vehicle ring; later a vocal Trump supporter.
  ([WAOK](https://waok.radio.com/blogs/on-point-with-juandolyn-stokes/angela-stanton-king-talks-adversity-and-support-for-trump))
  *Pardoned.*

**The pattern:** eleven acts of clemency, of which the majority went to people
convicted of public corruption or large-scale financial fraud — the offense
categories a president is least well positioned to be impartial about. The three
drug and property cases were the ones publicized.

Also in his final weeks: full pardons for four private security contractors

# January 20, 2025: the January 6 mass pardons

The first act of the second term, signed hours after the oath of office.

## What it covered

**Roughly 1,500 full pardons** — effectively everyone charged in connection with
the attack on the Capitol, including those who had pleaded guilty and those
still awaiting trial. Not a review of individual cases. A blanket.

**Fourteen commutations** for the leadership of the Oath Keepers and Proud
Boys — nine Oath Keepers and five Proud Boys — convicted of or charged with
**seditious conspiracy**, a charge so rarely used that prior to these cases the
Justice Department had brought it a handful of times in fifty years.

Among those released: **Stewart Rhodes**, Oath Keepers founder, who was serving
18 years; **Enrique Tarrio**, Proud Boys chairman, serving 22 years — the longest
sentence handed down in the entire investigation; and **Dominic Pezzola**,
**Joe Biggs**, and **Kelly Meggs**.

([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/20/g-s1-36809/trump-pardons-january-6-riot),
[CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/01/20/politics/january-6-2021-capitol-riot-pardons-trump/index.html),
[CNN, on what the pardons actually covered](https://www.cnn.com/2025/01/21/politics/what-to-know-pardons-january-6-trump/index.html),
[NPR, on what the recipients did](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/21/nx-s1-5268919/trump-issues-jan-6-pardons-attack-capitol-clemency),
[full list of those pardoned](https://www.jan-6.com/full-list-of-people-pardoned),
[GovFacts, five-year retrospective](https://govfacts.org/criminal-justice-public-safety/criminal-courts-legal-process/pardons-clemency/five-years-after-january-6-who-was-pardoned-and-what-happens-now/))

## The part that gets skipped

This was not a pardon of people who wandered through an open door and took
photographs. Those people existed and were charged accordingly. The blanket
covered them **and**:

- **More than 140 police officers were injured** during the seven-hour siege.
- The pardons covered the men who **beat D.C. Metropolitan Police officer
  Michael Fanone**, who was dragged into the crowd, tased, and suffered a heart
  attack.
- They covered those who **pepper-sprayed Capitol Police officer Brian
  Sicknick**, who died the following day.

Fanone's response:

> "Six individuals who assaulted me as I did my job on January 6 … will now walk
> free."

He added that his family was "less safe today because of Donald Trump and his
supporters."

U.S. Capitol Police Chief Thomas Manger said the pardons "send the message that
politics is more important than policing."

([Police1, officers injured on January 6 reflecting five years later](https://www.police1.com/Officer-Safety/officers-injured-during-jan-6-capitol-response-reflect-on-pardons-public-perception-5-years-later))

Congressional Republicans were, by their own account, blindsided — several had
publicly predicted that violent offenders would be excluded.
([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/5099016-trump-jan-6-pardons-divide-republicans/))

## What happened next

The obvious question about a blanket pardon is whether the people released were
in fact harmless. There is now five years of data.

- **At least 97 pardon recipients — roughly one in sixteen — have been tied to a

  [The 19th](https://19thnews.org/2026/01/january-6-pardons-arrests/))
- **CREW** documented at least 40 facing other criminal charges, with **at least
  12 alleged to have reoffended after receiving the pardon.**
  ([CREW](https://www.citizensforethics.org/reports-investigations/crew-reports/at-least-33-pardoned-insurrectionists-face-other-criminal-charges-but-many-are-now-going-free/),
  [earlier CREW count](https://www.citizensforethics.org/reports-investigations/crew-investigations/at-least-10-pardoned-insurrectionists-face-other-criminal-charges/))
- The new charges range from trespassing and drug possession to **stalking,
  fraud, plots to assassinate law enforcement and government officials, and
  homicide.**
One case: **Daniel Ball** was rearrested on a federal firearms charge within

**Why this is a point about the pardon and not just about the people.** A
blanket pardon does not review cases. Nobody sat down with these files and
decided these particular men deserved clemency — that is what *blanket* means.
The mechanism was: charged in connection with January 6, therefore released,
without regard to what else was in the file. David Daniel's CSAM charges were
discovered *by the January 6 investigation itself*.

The political movement that spent those years organized around the claim that
its opponents were running child sex rings issued a mass pardon that, as a
direct consequence of refusing to look at individual files, freed men who were
abusing children. One of them then tried to silence a child victim by promising
her a cut of his January 6 payout — the **$1.8 billion fund** described in
[Self-Enrichment](self-enrichment-and-ethics.md#13-the-18-billion-slush-fund).

For the longer-running version of this same gap between platform and conduct,
see [Republican Officials and Sex Crimes](gop-officials-and-sex-crimes.md).

## Erasing the convictions themselves

A pardon forgives a conviction; it does not erase the finding that the conduct
occurred. In **April 2026** the administration moved to go further, seeking to
**vacate the seditious conspiracy convictions outright.**
([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/15/nx-s1-5785318/trump-jan-6-capitol-riot-seditious-conspiracy),
[OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/04/15/trump-administration-moves-to-erase-jan-6-riot-convictions-for-seditious-conspiracy/))

The distinction is substantive. Clemency releases the person; vacatur attacks the
record — the jury's finding that a seditious conspiracy against the United States
occurred at all.

See also [Election Subversion and January 6](election-subversion-and-january-6.md)
for the attack itself and the $1.8 billion fund now paying January 6 defendants,
in [Self-Enrichment](self-enrichment-and-ethics.md#13-the-18-billion-slush-fund).

# Second term: clemency as a market

The February 2020 batch above was defensible as favoritism. What followed is
harder to characterize as anything but a price list.

## The finding

In August 2026, House Judiciary Committee Democrats released a staff report
titled **"Pardons, Inc.: How Trump and His Clemency-for-Cash Racket Let
White-Collar Criminals and International Drug Dealers Walk Free and Dodge
Billions in Restitution Owed to Their Victims."**

Its central claim: the traditional Justice Department clemency process — the
Office of the Pardon Attorney, its criteria, its review — has been sidelined and
replaced with a pay-to-play system in which wealthy offenders use political
contributions, Trump family business ties, and connected intermediaries to obtain
pardons.

**The number that matters: Trump's pardons have erased nearly $1.7 billion in
restitution, fines, and forfeitures owed to victims and taxpayers.**

([House Judiciary Democrats press release](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/new-judiciary-democrats-report-exposes-maga-s-cash-for-clemency-racket-which-gives-unrepentant-criminals-a-new-lease-on-life-and-leaves-victims-in-the-dust),
[the report itself, PDF](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2026-08-hjc-dems-staff-report-pardons-inc.pdf),
[pardon oversight letters, PDF](https://min.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/min.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/pardon_oversight_letters-merged.pdf))

**Standard caveat, applied here as everywhere:** that is a minority-staff report
written by political opponents. It is not a finding of fact by any court. What
makes it hard to wave off is the independent reporting underneath it.

## The independent reporting

**Reuters** reviewed thousands of pardon, lobbying, and campaign finance records
and identified **290 "influencers" who advocated 624 times across 197 successful
clemency bids** in the second term. Of those, 73 delivered more than one grant,
at least 110 are known Trump allies, and **11 had themselves received clemency
from Trump.**

A separate June 2026 Reuters review found that **96% of second-term clemency
grants failed to meet the Justice Department's longstanding guidelines** for such
requests.

**CBS News / 60 Minutes** documented the brokerage market directly: intermediaries
advertising proven access to the president's circle charge **as much as $2
million per engagement**, with some firms fielding offers as high as **$5
million** for a single case. The money is nominally for lobbying and advocacy,
not for a guaranteed outcome — which is precisely the structure that keeps it
legal.
([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-pardon-lobbyists-60-minutes/),
[full transcript](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-pardon-economy-60-minutes-transcript/),
[commentary](https://mynorthwest.com/kiro-opinion/trump-pardon-broker-industry/4277509))

Top Democrats have pledged to investigate the broker industry if they regain
control of Congress.
([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/democrats-pledge-investigation-trump-pardon-brokers/),
[Quartz](https://qz.com/democrats-probe-trump-pardon-brokers-60-minutes-091526))

### Changpeng Zhao — Binance

Pleaded guilty in 2023 to failures that enabled money laundering at the world's
largest crypto exchange; served four months. **Pardoned October 2025.**

5. Binance now holds **87% of USD1.**

Trump's account, on *60 Minutes*: he has "no idea who" Zhao is.

Zhao's account: "there's no business relationships whatsoever" between him and

## What is and is not established

**Established:** The money. The timing. The intermediaries and their fees. That
96% of grants failed the Justice Department's own criteria. That roughly $1.7
billion owed to defrauded victims and to the Treasury has been erased.

**Not established:** An agreement. Nobody has been charged with selling a pardon,
and no court has found a quid pro quo.

**Why that gap exists, and why it is not reassuring.** The pardon power is
textually unlimited and functionally unreviewable — the one presidential
authority with no check but impeachment. Proving a corrupt pardon requires
proving an agreement, and the agreement is the one part that never has to be
written down. Meanwhile the brokers are careful: the fee buys *advocacy*, not an
outcome, which keeps the transaction on the legal side of a bribery statute
regardless of what everyone involved understands it to be.

So the absence of charges is not evidence that nothing happened. It is what this
particular structure produces by design. What can be observed is the pattern, and
the pattern is: money in, clemency out, restitution to victims cancelled, at a
scale and hit rate that coincidence does not cover.
