# Timelines and Open Questions

**This file is explicitly speculative.** Everything else in this repository is
an attempt to state documented facts. This is an attempt to notice a pattern in
how those facts were arranged in time, and the honest status of a pattern is
*hypothesis*, not *finding*.

The dates below are all sourced. The inference drawn from them is not, and
cannot be. Read it as a question worth asking, and treat anyone who presents it
as settled — including the original author of this list — as overreaching.

## The pattern being proposed

Trump repeatedly took drastic foreign-policy actions — actions that cost lives,
mostly non-American lives — at moments of maximum domestic legal pressure, and
each one consumed the news cycle that had been covering that pressure.

Correlation is not causation, and a president has a great many reasons to act on
any given day. But the sequencing in both cases below is tight enough to be
worth putting on the record.

---

## Case 1: Abandoning the Kurds, October 2019

**September 24, 2019** — Speaker Pelosi announces that six House committees will
begin a formal impeachment inquiry.

**October 8, 2019** — The White House sends House Democratic leaders an
eight-page letter refusing all cooperation with what it calls a "partisan and
unconstitutional inquiry."
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/10/08/us/politics/sondland-trump-ukraine-impeach.html))
Legal analysts widely read the letter as a political document rather than a
legal one — it made essentially no legal argument.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/10/09/us/politics/trump-case-against-impeachment.html),
[Roll Call](https://www.rollcall.com/news/former-ethics-czar-warns-impeachment-letter-mistakes-trump-king))

**October 13, 2019** — Five days later, Trump orders the withdrawal of U.S.
forces from northern Syria.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/10/13/us/politics/mark-esper-syria-kurds-turkey.html))

**The consequences:**

- More than a hundred ISIS prisoners escaped custody.
  ([ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/US/100-isis-prisoners-loose-security-officials-act-now/story?id=66499973))
- Turkey's offensive killed hundreds and displaced thousands of the Kurdish
  fighters and civilians who had done the ground fighting against ISIS on
  America's behalf.
  ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-syria-security-turkey-usa/thousands-flee-hundreds-reported-dead-in-turkish-attack-on-us-allied-kurds-in-syria-idUSKBN1WP0VH))
- America's value as a coalition partner took lasting damage.
  ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2019/10/18/trump-can-forget-burden-sharing-now/))

**The question:** Did the abandonment of the Kurds, ordered five days after the
White House declared open war on the impeachment inquiry, serve in part to move
that inquiry off the front page?

## Case 2: The Soleimani strike, January 2020

The fight in this window was specifically about **witnesses** — whether the
Senate trial would hear from anyone who could describe what Trump did.

**Thursday, December 19–20, 2019** — Pelosi signals she will delay transmitting
the articles of impeachment to the Senate, seeking clarity on trial rules and
potentially pushing proceedings into the new year.
([The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/articles/after-house-impeaches-trump-timing-of-next-steps-is-unclear-11576755097))

**December 21, 2019** — Bill Kristol's "Republicans for the Rule of Law" runs an
ad urging viewers to call their senators and demand that senior Trump officials
be compelled to testify.
([HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/republicans-for-the-rule-of-law-impeachment-witnesses_n_5dfec4afe4b0843d35fd0c00?))

**December 25, 2019** — Trump spends Christmas night attacking Pelosi on
Twitter.
([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/475928-trump-lashes-out-at-pelosi-in-christmas-tweets-decries-scam-impeachment))

> Why should Crazy Nancy Pelosi, just because she has a slight majority in the
> House, be allowed to Impeach the President of the United States? Got ZERO
> Republican votes, there was no crime, the call with Ukraine was perfect, with
> "no pressure." She said it must be "bipartisan & overwhelming," but this Scam
> Impeachment was neither. Also, very unfair with no Due Process, proper
> representation, or witnesses. Now Pelosi is demanding everything the
> Republicans weren't allowed to have in the House. Dems want to run majority
> Republican Senate. Hypocrites!

The claim rests on two errors. There is no bipartisanship requirement for
impeachment, and an impeachment is not a criminal trial: it is a political
process with a different standard of proof, different rules, and a different
remedy.
([The Washington Post, on the differences](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2020/01/22/4-big-differences-between-senate-impeachment-trial-criminal-trial))

**Thursday, January 2, 2020** — Unredacted emails surface showing the order to
withhold Ukraine aid came directly from the president.
([The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/trump-impeachment-emails-ukraine-aid-schumer-leak-pentagon-a9268391.html))

- Trump had confirmed freezing the assistance before pressing Zelensky to
  investigate Biden — contradicting his claim that he withheld it out of concern
  about Ukrainian corruption.
  ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/story/2019/09/24/donald-trump-ukraine-military-aid-1509070))
- It also disposed of the "Obama did the same thing" defense; Obama's aid pauses
  followed the statutory process this one bypassed.
  ([Senate Appropriations Committee minority](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/news/minority/fact-check-president-trumps-false-claim-equating-his-illegal-ukraine-aid-freeze-with-president-obamas-lawful-legitimate-pauses-on-aid))
- The freeze appears to have violated the Impoundment Control Act.
  ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2019/dec/05/did-trump-violate-impoundment-control-act/))

  The Impoundment Control Act exists because Nixon impounded appropriated funds
  to defund programs he disliked, and Congress passed it in 1974 to stop exactly
  that. A president being caught doing the thing the post-Watergate statute was
  written to prevent, in the middle of his own impeachment, is the kind of irony
  that should be hard to ignore. It was ignored.

**Friday, January 3, 2020** — The next day, the U.S. military kills Qassem
Soleimani in an airstrike at Baghdad International Airport, at Trump's
direction — an act of war against a state the U.S. was not at war with.
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/01/trump-assassination-soleimani-200103172526102.html))

Coverage of the witness fight vanished into coverage of a possible war with
Iran.

**Monday, January 6, 2020** — John Bolton announces he will testify if
subpoenaed.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/01/06/us/politics/bolton-testify-impeachment-trial.html))

**Friday, January 10, 2020** — Trump signals he will invoke executive privilege
to block Bolton's testimony.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/01/06/us/politics/bolton-testify-impeachment-trial.html))

*(The citation above is to the January 6 report; the January 10 development
lacks a distinct source in this repository.)*

**The question:** Did the United States kill a foreign state official — itself an
act of war and an extraordinary assertion of executive power — the day after
documentary proof of the president's personal culpability leaked, and days
before the fight over whether witnesses would appear?

## What can and cannot be concluded

**Established:** The dates. The order of events. That each foreign action
immediately displaced coverage of a specific, imminent legal threat.

**Not established:** Intent. No document, testimony, or admission ties either
military decision to the impeachment calendar. A president facing impeachment
still receives intelligence briefings, still faces real decisions, and would
plausibly have made some version of both choices anyway.

**The honest position:** This is a pattern worth naming and a question worth
asking out loud. It is not proof, and calling it proof would put it in the same
category as the things this repository exists to argue against.

---

## On the standard of proof applied here

An argument that cannot be lost is an argument that was never about evidence.
The sections above are constructed so that they can be lost: each states what the
documents establish, what they do not, and which inferences remain unsupported.

The material in this repository either holds up against its sources or it does
not. Nothing else is claimed for it.
