# Election Subversion and January 6

The attempt to overturn the 2020 election, the attack on the Capitol, and — in
2026 — the use of federal law enforcement against the people who investigated it.

## 1. Georgia: "find 11,780 votes"

**January 2, 2021.** Trump telephoned Georgia Secretary of State Brad
Raffensperger — a Republican — and pressured him to reverse the state's
certified result:

> "I just want to find 11,780 votes."

The number was not arbitrary. It was Biden's margin in Georgia, plus one.

The call was recorded. Raffensperger released the recording. Trump has never
disputed that he said it; his defense has been that the call was "perfect."

**The criminal case.** A Fulton County special grand jury investigated, and in
August 2023 a regular grand jury charged Trump and 18 allies under Georgia's
RICO statute with a criminal conspiracy to reverse the state's 2020 result.

## 2. Pressuring the Justice Department

**December 2020.** Trump repeatedly pressured senior Justice Department
leadership to declare the election corrupt — "just say the election was corrupt
and leave the rest to me and the Republican Congressmen."

When they refused, he attempted to install **Jeffrey Clark**, a loyalist
environmental lawyer, as acting Attorney General, for the purpose of sending
letters urging swing states to overturn their certified results.

The plan collapsed only because the entire senior leadership of the department
threatened to resign en masse.

The plan was not stopped by law, by a court, or by an institutional safeguard.
It was stopped by a specific group of individuals deciding to resign rather than
execute it — a contingent outcome, not a structural one.

## 3. January 6, 2021

Following months of false fraud claims, Trump directed a crowd of supporters to
the Capitol as Congress met to count the electoral votes. The resulting riot
disrupted the certification of a presidential election for the first time in
American history.

He was impeached a second time, for incitement of insurrection — the only
president ever impeached twice. The Senate acquitted, with 57 votes to convict,
10 short of the two-thirds required. Seven Republicans voted to convict.

Minority Leader Mitch McConnell, having voted to acquit on jurisdictional
grounds, stated on the Senate floor that Trump was "practically and morally
responsible for provoking the events of the day."

**The federal case.** In August 2023, Special Counsel Jack Smith indicted Trump
on four federal felony counts for a multi-part conspiracy to defraud the United
States and obstruct the certification of the 2020 election. The case was
abandoned after the 2024 election under Justice Department policy against
prosecuting a sitting president.

**The pardons.** On January 20, 2025 — hours after taking the oath — Trump
issued roughly **1,500 pardons** covering nearly everyone charged in connection
with the attack, plus **14 commutations** for the Oath Keepers and Proud Boys
leadership convicted of seditious conspiracy. More than 140 officers had been
injured that day; the pardons covered the men who beat one of them and
pepper-sprayed another who died the next day. In April 2026 the administration
moved to vacate the seditious conspiracy convictions themselves.

The full accounting — who was released, what they have done since, and the
fourteen later charged with sex crimes — is in
[Pardons and Clemency](pardons-and-clemency.md#january-20-2025-the-january-6-mass-pardons).

The report on that investigation has not been fully released. The argument for
releasing it:
[The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/29/opinion/jack-smith-report-trump.html#1)
— "Fine, Don't Prosecute the President. But Release Jack Smith's Report."

## 4. The reversal: 2026

Having been investigated for trying to seize Georgia's election records, Trump's
own FBI seized Georgia's election records.

**In 2026, the FBI raided Fulton County election offices**, taking 2020 ballots,
voter rolls, and scanner images — the evidence in the case against him, removed
by the agency he controls, ahead of the 2026 midterms.

The Justice Department has also pursued a "grand conspiracy" investigation
targeting the officials who investigated the 2016 Russia interference and its
aftermath, continuing with subpoenas after the lead prosecutor's departure.
([AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-russia-cia-justice-department-ee73be07c316ac9a27f18b4a5527f5a6?__vfz=medium%3Dstandalone_top_pages))

**Why this section exists.** Sections 1 through 3 describe an attempt to overturn
an election that failed, largely because state officials and career prosecutors
refused. Section 4 describes what happened to those safeguards afterward. The
first three are history. The fourth is the reason the first three matter.

Set alongside the mass pardons and the $1.8 billion fund now compensating January
6 defendants, the through-line is not difficult: everyone who acted on his behalf
that day has been released and is being paid, and the records of the attempt are
being seized or vacated.

## A note on what is and is not sourced here

The source compilation these entries came from carried few citations for the
2020–2021 material, on the apparent assumption that it is common knowledge. Most
of it is: the Raffensperger recording is public, the impeachment votes are in the
Congressional Record, and the indictments are public court filings.

The gap remains. The primary documents for sections 1–3 are the Fulton County
indictment, the Smith indictment, the House January 6 Committee's final report,
and the Senate impeachment roll call; none is currently linked here.
Contributions adding them are welcome.

See also [Legal Record](legal-record.md) for the case-by-case status of each
prosecution, and [Timeline](timeline.md) for where these events sit in the
sequence.
