## The convictions and liability findings

### Hush money — 34 felony counts (May 2024)

Convicted on 34 counts of falsifying business records to conceal a $130,000
payment to Stormy Daniels made days before the 2016 election. The payment went
through Michael Cohen; the cover-up involved falsified records and a
reimbursement scheme run while Trump was president.

A federal judge later rejected the attempt to void the conviction on presidential
immunity grounds, holding that "paying hush money to an adult film star or
engaging in a cover-up of an embarrassment are not subsumed in presidential
immunity."

**The only criminal conviction of a U.S. president in American history.**

Details of the underlying scheme:
[Impeachment and Obstruction](impeachment-and-obstruction.md#4-campaign-finance-felonies-and-the-hush-money-scheme).

### New York civil fraud (2024)

A New York judge found Trump, his adult sons, and ten of his companies liable
for fraud after the Attorney General documented **200 instances of fraudulent
asset valuations**. The finding: the defendants "made up valuations" to obtain
favorable loan and insurance terms, netting Trump over $100 million.

The initial penalty was roughly $500 million with interest. An appellate court
later threw out the financial penalty. **The liability finding was not
disturbed.**

That distinction gets elided in both directions. He did not "win" — a court found
that his business empire's valuations were fraudulent, and that finding stands.
He also is not paying $500 million.

### Trump Organization tax fraud (2021–22)

The company and its longtime CFO Allen Weisselberg were indicted and convicted
over a 15-year scheme to compensate executives off the books to evade taxes.
Weisselberg served time. The company was fined.

### E. Jean Carroll (2023–24)

Two civil juries found Trump liable for sexually abusing Carroll in a Bergdorf
Goodman dressing room in the mid-1990s and for repeatedly defaming her
afterward. Awards totaled over $88 million. The presiding judge subsequently
wrote that the jury's finding was, in ordinary usage, a finding of rape.

See [Sexual Misconduct Allegations](sexual-misconduct.md).

### Trump University (2016)

Shut down in 2011 amid allegations of "misleading, fraudulent and dishonest"
sales tactics that pushed students into paying up to $35,000 for courses that
never delivered the promised mentorship or access. New York Attorney General
Eric Schneiderman sued Trump personally. Settled for **$25 million** covering
more than 6,000 victims — paid in the weeks between the election and the
inauguration, after years of insisting he would never settle.

A New York judge had already found Trump personally liable for operating the
enterprise without the required license.
([Xinhua, on the settlement](http://www.xinhuanet.com//english/2016-11/19/c_135842360.htm#1))

### The Trump Foundation (2018–19)

Dissolved under court supervision after the New York Attorney General documented
a "shocking pattern of illegality": charitable funds used to settle Trump's
personal lawsuits, to buy a portrait of himself, and to support his 2016
campaign in coordination with campaign staff. $2 million in damages ordered, plus
distribution of remaining assets. Trump was barred from leading any New York
charity; his children were required to undergo training.

He had publicly pledged more than $8.5 million to charities over 15 years and
delivered about a third of it.
([Mother Jones, on the broader pattern](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2025/10/trump-230-million-justice-department-settlement-charity-grift/?utm_source=mj-newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily-newsletter-10-23-2025))

More: [Business Record and Fraud](business-and-fraud.md#4-the-trump-foundation).

## The cases that ended without a verdict

These matter precisely because they did not conclude. A dismissal on procedural
grounds is not a finding of innocence, and saying so is not spin — it is what
the dismissals say.

### Classified documents (indicted June 2023)

The Justice Department charged Trump under the Espionage Act with retaining and
refusing to return classified material after leaving office. The indictment
alleged he stored boxes of top-secret records — including material on U.S.
defense and weapons capabilities and nuclear programs — in a bathroom and shower
at Mar-a-Lago, lied about having them, showed them to people without clearances,
and obstructed the FBI's efforts to retrieve them. He was charged alongside his
valet and a property manager.

**Dismissed on procedural grounds** (the validity of the special counsel's
appointment) after he won the 2024 election. The conduct was never tried.

### Federal election interference (indicted August 2023)

Four felony counts from Special Counsel Jack Smith for a multi-part conspiracy
to overturn the 2020 election. **Abandoned** after the 2024 election under
Justice Department policy against prosecuting a sitting president.

See [Election Subversion and January 6](election-subversion-and-january-6.md).

### Georgia RICO (indicted August 2023)

Trump and 18 co-defendants charged under Georgia's racketeering statute. Several
co-defendants pleaded guilty. The case against Trump has not been tried, and in
2026 the FBI seized the underlying election records from Fulton County.

### The Russia investigation

**State this one accurately, because the accurate version is strong enough.**

Special Counsel Robert Mueller's investigation **did not establish that Trump or
his campaign conspired or coordinated with the Russian government.** That is what
the report says, and anyone claiming otherwise is misrepresenting it.

What the investigation did establish:

- Russia interfered in the 2016 election "in sweeping and systematic fashion."
- The Trump campaign "expected it would benefit electorally" from that
  interference.
- There were numerous contacts between the campaign and Russian-affiliated
  individuals, including multiple offers of assistance.
- It produced criminal convictions of several of Trump's closest advisers —
  Manafort, Gates, Flynn, Cohen, Papadopoulos, Stone.
- On obstruction of justice, Mueller **declined to exonerate**, explicitly:
  "while this report does not conclude that the President committed a crime, it
  also does not exonerate him."

Newly revealed memos also show the FBI opened a counterintelligence inquiry into
whether Trump was "wittingly or unwittingly" acting on Russia's behalf, after he
fired James Comey.
([New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/08/05/us-news/fbi-opened-probe-into-whether-trump-was-russian-agent-after-he-fired-james-comey-newly-revealed-memos-show/#1),
[The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/trump-fbi-russia-2016-investigation-b3028230.html#1),
[Daily Express](https://www.express.co.uk/news/world/2236398/bombshell-fbi-documents-show-trump#comments-wrapper#1),
[Koha](https://www.koha.net/en/bote/dokumentet-e-fbi-se-zbulojne-se-trumpi-u-hetua-si-agjent-rus#1),
[Legalbrief, on the report's delivery to the AG](https://legalbrief.co.za/diary/legalbrief-today/story/ag-gets-mueller-report-on-trump-russia-probe/print/))

**A common overstatement:** that the investigation established the campaign
accepted foreign help. It did not establish a conspiracy or coordination, and
characterizing it that way misstates the report. The contacts, the expectation of
benefit, the convictions, and the obstruction findings are all documented; the
conspiracy finding is not.

**A counter-argument in circulation:**
[The Federalist](https://thefederalist.com/2026/04/15/new-documents-reveal-democrats-plot-to-frame-trump-with-ukraine-call/#1)
contends the Ukraine case was manufactured. It is listed here so the claim can be
read and assessed directly.

### The first impeachment

Trump's July 25, 2019 call with Volodymyr Zelensky, in which he tied
congressionally appropriated military aid to the announcement of an investigation
of Joe Biden. The White House's own released memorandum of the call confirms he
urged the investigation. Impeached for abuse of power and obstruction of
Congress; acquitted by the Senate.
([RNZ, on the call memo](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/399619/trump-impeachment-memo-confirms-president-urged-biden-inquiry#1),
[RNZ, on the Attorney General question](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/402805/trump-denies-asking-attorney-general-to-clear-him-on-ukraine#1),
[ABC Australia retrospective](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-12-18/the-lessons-from-donald-trump-s-first-impeachment-trial/106137900#1))

## The pattern across the table

Across the outcome column, the cases that reached a verdict were lost. The cases
that did not reach a verdict ended for reasons unrelated to the evidence — a
settlement, a procedural ruling, an election, a Justice Department policy
regarding sitting presidents.

There is no row in which a trier of fact examined the evidence and found in his
favor.
