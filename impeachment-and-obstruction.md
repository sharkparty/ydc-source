## 1. The Ukraine extortion scheme

### The core of it

Trump withheld $400 million in congressionally appropriated military aid to
Ukraine in order to pressure Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky into
announcing an investigation into Joe Biden, his likeliest 2020 opponent. The
allegations he wanted investigated had already been examined and discredited,
and he knew it. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-50886437))

The House of Representatives impeached him for it on December 18, 2019.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/12/18/us/politics/trump-impeached.html))

Withholding the aid also appears to have violated the Impoundment Control Act,
which bars a president from unilaterally refusing to spend money Congress has
appropriated.
([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2019/dec/05/did-trump-violate-impoundment-control-act/))
His defense — that Obama had done the same thing — was false; Obama's pauses on
aid followed the statutory process this one skipped.
([Senate Appropriations Committee minority fact check](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/news/minority/fact-check-president-trumps-false-claim-equating-his-illegal-ukraine-aid-freeze-with-president-obamas-lawful-legitimate-pauses-on-aid))

Overview of the whole affair:
[Trump–Ukraine scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump%E2%80%93Ukraine_scandal).

### Timeline

**April 25, 2019** — Joe Biden announces his candidacy and immediately takes a
commanding lead in Democratic primary polling.

**Early May 2019** — Within one to two weeks, the pressure campaign is in
motion. It is run publicly by Rudy Giuliani and, per John Bolton's manuscript,
directed by Trump himself, who looped in his National Security Advisor, acting
Chief of Staff, and White House Counsel.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/01/31/us/politics/trump-bolton-ukraine.html))

> President Trump directed John R. Bolton, then his national security adviser,
> to help with his pressure campaign to extract damaging information on
> Democrats from Ukrainian officials, according to an unpublished manuscript by
> Mr. Bolton. … Mr. Trump gave the instruction, Mr. Bolton wrote, during an Oval
> Office conversation in early May that included the acting White House chief of
> staff, Mick Mulvaney, the president's personal lawyer Rudolph W. Giuliani and
> the White House counsel, Pat A. Cipollone, who is now leading the president's
> impeachment defense.

Bolton's account further has Trump instructing him to call Zelensky directly to
guarantee that Zelensky would meet with Giuliani — meaning the extortion was
aimed at Zelensky from the outset, not improvised later.

**May 9, 2019** — Giuliani describes his own activities to *The New York Times*
in terms that would be difficult to improve on as a confession.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/05/09/us/politics/giuliani-ukraine-trump.html))

> "We're not meddling in an election, we're meddling in an investigation, which
> we have a right to do. There's nothing illegal about it," he said. "Somebody
> could say it's improper. And this isn't foreign policy — I'm asking them to do
> an investigation that they're doing already and that other people are telling
> them to stop. And I'm going to give them reasons why they shouldn't stop it
> because that information will be very, very helpful to my client, and may turn
> out to be helpful to my government."

**May 10, 2019** — Giuliani sends Zelensky a letter stating that he is acting
with Trump's knowledge and consent. The letter surfaced on Lev Parnas's phone
and was transmitted to the House Judiciary Committee as new evidence.
([House Intelligence Committee transmittal letter, PDF](https://intelligence.house.gov/uploadedfiles/20200114_-_hpsci_transmittal_letter_to_hjc_-_new_evidence.pdf))

> Dear President-Elect Zelensky:
>
> I am private counsel to President Donald J. Trump. Just to be precise, I
> represent him as a private citizen, not as President of the United States.
> This is quite common under American law because the duties and privileges of a
> President and a private citizen are not the same. Separate representation is
> usual process.
>
> […]
>
> However, I have a more specific request. **In my capacity as personal counsel
> to President Trump and with his knowledge and consent**, I request a meeting
> with you on this upcoming Monday, May 13th or Tuesday, May 14th. I will need
> no more than a half-hour of your time and I will be accompanied by my
> colleague Victoria Toensing, a distinguished American attorney who is very
> familiar with this matter.

Parnas texted a copy of the letter to a close aide of Zelensky's shortly after
it was drafted. The letter makes plain that the trip Giuliani publicly described
as "meddling in an investigation" was undertaken with the president's knowledge
and support.

**The aftermath** — The Senate acquitted without calling Bolton or Giuliani as
witnesses. Trump subsequently discussed the affair freely in interviews. The
defense was never really a denial; it was an assertion of impunity.

**Context:** For the argument that the January 2020 Soleimani strike was timed
against the witness fight, see
[Timelines and Open Questions](timelines-and-open-questions.md).

## 2. Soliciting foreign election help in public

Having been impeached over a covert solicitation, Trump stood on the White House
lawn and asked China, on camera, to investigate the Bidens. The distinguishing
feature versus Ukraine is only that the United States was not withholding $400
million in military aid from China at the time.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-49924579))

Soliciting anything of value from a foreign national in connection with a U.S.
election is a federal crime:

> *52 U.S. Code § 30121 — Contributions and donations by foreign nationals*
> ([Cornell Law School, Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/52/30121))

## 3. Obstruction of the Russia investigation

The Special Counsel's report is the primary source for the items in this
section: [*Report on the Investigation into Russian Interference in the 2016
Presidential Election*, U.S. Department of Justice (PDF)](https://www.justice.gov/storage/report.pdf).
A readable overview: [Mueller Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mueller_Report)
(encyclopedia summary, not itself a source).

- **Pressuring Comey to drop the Flynn investigation.** That investigation was
  examining whether there was a link between Russia and Trump's own campaign —
  which makes the request an abuse of executive power and an act of obstruction,
  not a policy preference.
  ([Mueller Report](https://www.justice.gov/storage/report.pdf))

- **Firing Comey when he did not drop it.**
  ([Mueller Report](https://www.justice.gov/storage/report.pdf))

- **Attempting to fire Mueller**, the man appointed to take over the
  investigation after Comey's dismissal.
  ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/01/27/581343525/legal-or-not-trump-s-attempt-to-fire-mueller-falls-into-a-pattern-of-intention-t))

- **Directing members of his administration to lie to investigators**, including
  instructing White House Counsel Don McGahn to deny the order to fire Mueller.
  ([The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/mueller-testimony-trump-don-mcgahn-lie-karen-bass-a9018956.html))

- **What the investigation found about 2016.**

  The report states that the investigation **"did not establish"** that Trump or
  his campaign conspired or coordinated with the Russian government. Claims that
  it established the campaign accepted foreign help misstate the finding.

  What it did establish:

  - Russia interfered in the 2016 election "in sweeping and systematic fashion."
  - The campaign "expected it would benefit electorally" from that interference.
  - There were numerous contacts with Russian-affiliated individuals, including
    multiple offers of assistance — among them the Trump Tower meeting, taken
    after Donald Trump Jr. was told the material came "from Russia and its
    government," to which he replied "if it's what you say I love it."
  - Six close associates were convicted.

  On obstruction, Mueller declined to clear him, and said so in terms:

  > "If we had had confidence that the president clearly didn't commit a crime,
  > we would have said so." — Robert Mueller

  > "Under longstanding department policy, a president cannot be charged with a
  > federal crime while he is in office." — Robert Mueller

  Full breakdown, including the later-revealed FBI counterintelligence inquiry
  into whether he was acting on Russia's behalf:
  [Legal Record](legal-record.md#the-russia-investigation).

- **Cheating in the 2016 election and then obstructing the investigation of that
  election** — including the question of how Mueller himself became a subject of
  the obstruction inquiry.
  ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/how-mueller-himself-factors-into-the-trump-obstruction-investigation))

## 4. Campaign-finance felonies and the hush-money scheme

- **Implicated in at least 11 felonies by his own former attorney**, Michael
  Cohen. ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en_us/article/8xykv4/michael-cohen-implicated-trump-in-at-least-11-different-felonies))

- **Directed an illegal scheme to pay hush money to conceal an affair** during
  the 2016 campaign — the offense Cohen pleaded guilty to, carried out at
  Trump's direction and reimbursed from Trump's personal account while he was
  president. ([Vox](https://www.vox.com/2019/2/27/18243227/cohen-testimony-trump-stormy-daniels-payment))

  > "I am providing a copy of a $35,000 check that President Trump personally
  > signed from his personal bank account on August 1 of 2017 — when he was
  > president of the United States — pursuant to the cover-up, which was the
  > basis of my guilty plea, to reimburse me — the word used by Mr. Trump's TV
  > lawyer — for the illegal hush money I paid on his behalf," Cohen said. "This
  > $35,000 check was one of 11 check installments that was paid throughout the
  > year, while he was president."

  **Context:** In May 2024 a New York jury convicted Trump on 34 felony counts
  of falsifying business records arising from this same scheme — the first
  criminal conviction of a U.S. president. That verdict postdates the original
  compilation of this list.
