## Cold open

**#153** Renée Good. Thirty-seven. A mother of three. A federal immigration agent
==shot==^[✏️ caption: "sh0t"] her three times during a
traffic stop. The medical examiner ruled it a homicide. And you didn't care.

## The run

**#154** You didn't care when federal agents ==killed==^[✏️ caption: "k!lled"]
Alex Pretti, an ICU nurse, at a protest. Video shows an agent taking his gun away
before he was shot. You built your whole identity around the Second Amendment,
and when an armed citizen was disarmed and then shot by federal agents, you said
he should've complied.

**#156** You didn't care that the president's entire response was that it
"should have not happened." Four words for two dead citizens.

**#136** You didn't care about "Alligator Alcatraz," with thirty-plus people to
a cage at nearly a hundred degrees. Amnesty's report on it is called *Torture and
Enforced Disappearances*. You thought the name was funny.

**#137** You didn't care that a private prison company's affiliate gave his super
PAC one point four million dollars after getting a hundred and sixty-five million
in detention contracts.

**#120** You didn't care that a proposal for a private deportation army, with
"processing camps" on military bases, got multiple meetings at the White House.

**#141** You didn't care that two federal judges found his troop deployments on
American streets partly illegal. You stockpiled rifles for thirty years for fear
of exactly this, then cheered when it came.

## Closer

**#155** And you didn't care that Operation Metro Surge left at least nine people
dead, nobody charged, and the evidence held back from Minnesota for six months
until the state sued. The federal government argued state prosecutors have no
power over federal agents. What happened to states' rights? Is this small
government? Is this the most transparent administration ever?
