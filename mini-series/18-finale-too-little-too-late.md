# E18: Too Little, Too Late

> [!info] Episode card
> **Receipts:** 1–185 (montage) · **Master list:** [[you-didnt-care]] ·
> **Series:** [[00-series-index]]
> **Previous:** [[17-the-kids-in-minab]]

> [!question] Outro not decided
> You said you're not sold on the outro. Below are three options with different
> endings. None of them is final. Pick one, combine them, or throw them all out.

## The montage (all options)

A rapid-fire run of receipt numbers and one-line captions: **1, 2, 3… 185**,
each on screen for a few frames, accelerating, while you say nothing. It ends
on a hard cut to black with **"185."**

Then, to camera:

> One hundred and eighty-five. That's not everything. That's just what we could
> source. You lived through every one of them, same as us. And you didn't care.

## Option A: The gas pump

> You didn't care about the kids in Minab, or Renée Good, or the women, or the
> ballots, or the pardons. You didn't care when it was somebody else's mother or
> somebody else's country. And one day it'll be the price at the pump that
> finally gets you to care. We'll still be here. We'll still have the receipts.
> It'll still be too late.

*Closest to the TikTok that inspired this. The payoff is the one thing they
finally cared about. The risk is that it lands on them, not on your audience.*

## Option B: We noticed

> This series was never really for them. It was for you, the ones who noticed.
> Who read the headlines at 2 a.m. Who lost the argument at Thanksgiving because
> facts don't win arguments with people who don't care. You weren't crazy. You
> weren't alone. There are one hundred eighty-five receipts, and there are
> millions of us.

*The solidarity ending. It lands on your audience and gives them something to
share. The risk is that it's softer than the rest of the series.*

## Option C: Too little, too late

> Somewhere around receipt one hundred, the rest of us stopped waiting for you
> to care. So if you care now, good. But you don't get to be surprised. You were
> handed every one of these, and you chose him every time. Whatever it costs you
> now, the job, the friendships, the family dinners that got quiet, you paid for
> it one receipt at a time.

*The version closest to what you described. It frames the losses as the price of
their choices, not as a statement about any one person's life, so nobody can
fact-check it.*

## Sign-off (for whichever option)

> We noticed. We were here for all of it. You were never alone.
> Every receipt, sourced: link in bio.
