# The Epstein Files

What the released documents establish, what they do not, and what the Justice
Department kept back.

## Qualifications that govern this page

Bad sourcing does more damage on this subject than on any other in this
repository, because the documented record is serious and the widely circulated
version of it is frequently wrong. Three constraints apply throughout:

1. **Being named in the files is not evidence of a crime.** The files are the
   investigative record of a decades-long federal case. They contain victim
   interviews, tips, news clippings, flight logs, and unverified allegations
   submitted by members of the public. Appearing in them means only that a
   document mentions the person.
2. **Trump has not been charged with any offense related to Epstein.** The
   Justice Department has stated that some claims against him in the files were
   "untrue and sensationalist," submitted to the FBI shortly before the 2020
   election.
3. **The strongest material here is not the mention counts.** It is the
   Department's handling of the files after the law required their release.

## 1. The claim about mention counts, corrected

**The circulating claim — that Trump appears in the Epstein files more than
Epstein does — does not hold up, and should not be used.**

No published analysis makes that comparison, and it is implausible on its face:
these are Epstein's own investigative and prosecution files, in which his name is
the organizing subject of nearly every document. The claim appears to be a
garbled version of a real headline comparison — that Trump is mentioned more
often in the files than God is mentioned in the Bible — which is a rhetorical
flourish, not an analytic finding.

**Related and also unsupported:** Rep. Jamie Raskin's statement that Trump is
named "more than a million times" in the unredacted files. Public tallies and
independent document indexes do not corroborate it; every published count is
orders of magnitude lower.
([Truthout, reporting the claim](https://truthout.org/articles/raskin-trump-mentioned-more-than-a-million-times-in-unredacted-epstein-files/))

### What the counts actually are

| Measure | Count | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| References to "Trump, his wife, his Mar-a-Lago club… and other related words and phrases" in the January 2026 release | **38,000+** | New York Times review |
| Individual files flagged as mentioning Trump | **5,300+** | New York Times |
| Times Trump is mentioned across the 3 million documents | **1,000+** | CNN |
| Hits for "Donald Trump" on the DOJ's Epstein site | **1,800+** | DOJ site search |

([The New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/post/206023/how-often-donald-trump-mentioned-epstein-files),
[NYT analysis, summarized](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/york-times-uncovers-5-300-231021336.html),
[the 38,000 figure](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/often-trump-comes-latest-epstein-165218827.html),
[CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/01/31/politics/new-documents-trump-epstein))

The first figure is the one most often quoted without its definition. The 38,000
count is not 38,000 mentions of Donald Trump. It is every hit for a set of
related search terms — his name, Melania Trump's, Mar-a-Lago, and other
associated phrases — across three million pages. A large share are **news
articles mentioning Trump during his presidency that Epstein forwarded to
others**, together with Epstein's own commentary about him to journalists and
associates.

The volume is a relevant fact about Epstein's preoccupations. It is not 38,000
items of evidence, and it collapses under scrutiny when presented as such.

## 2. What the files do document about the relationship

Trump's account has been that he "wasn't a fan" of Epstein's and "knew him like
everybody in Palm Beach knew him." The documents do not support that
characterization.

- **Flight records** place Trump on Epstein's private aircraft in the 1990s.
  ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/latest-epstein-document-release-includes-multiple-trump-mentions),
  [NPR](https://npr.org/2025/12/23/nx-s1-5653302/more-epstein-files-released-trump-mentioned),
  [Axios](https://axios.com/2025/12/23/doj-new-epstein-files-trump/))
- Substantial evidence indicates the two were **friendly for years** before a
  falling-out Trump has placed in the mid-2000s.
- **A newly surfaced document undercuts Trump's claim not to have known about
  Epstein's conduct** — the central claim of his defense.
  ([CNN analysis](https://www.cnn.com/2026/02/11/politics/epstein-files-trump-claims-contradictions-analysis))
- **An Epstein victim told the FBI that Ghislaine Maxwell once "presented her"
  to Trump at a party and indicated she was "available."** The same witness told
  the FBI that nothing happened between her and Trump.
  ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/01/31/politics/new-documents-trump-epstein))

The witness's own qualification is reproduced alongside the allegation because
it forms part of the record. Citing the allegation without it would misrepresent
the source.

### The claims that did not survive the files

CNN's running comparison of administration statements against the documents:

- **July 2025:** Trump denied having been told his name was in the files. It
  later emerged that **Attorney General Pam Bondi had told him precisely that in
  May.**
- Officials including Trump and Bondi made repeated public characterizations of
  the files' contents that the releases subsequently undercut.
- The administration promised release, then reversed, then was compelled by
  statute.

## 3. How the files came out

**The Epstein Files Transparency Act** directs the Justice Department to release,
with narrow exceptions, all documents, records, videos, and images from the
Epstein and Maxwell investigations and prosecutions — unclassified material
within 30 days.

- **House: 427–1.** The sole no vote was Rep. Clay Higgins.
- **Senate: passed by unanimous consent**, hours later, without recorded debate.
- **Signed by Trump on November 19, 2025**, after months of resisting release.

([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2025/11/trump-signs-epstein-files-transparency-act-into-law/),
[the Act, PDF](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1426091/dl),
[Britannica summary](https://www.britannica.com/question/Did-Donald-Trump-sign-a-bill-to-release-the-Epstein-files),
[House Oversight Democrats](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/ranking-member-robert-garcia-statement-house-passing-epstein-files-transparency),
[Epstein Files Transparency Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_Files_Transparency_Act))

The margin is the notable fact. On a question bearing directly on the president,
essentially the entire Republican conference voted against his position — a
near-unprecedented breach in this period, and a measure of how untenable
continued withholding had become.

**The releases:** the Justice Department published roughly **3.5 million pages**
in compliance, including some 3 million documents, 2,000 videos, and 180,000
images, and stated that this was the final release.
([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-publishes-35-million-responsive-pages-compliance-epstein-files),
[CBS News live coverage](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/epstein-files-released-doj-2026/),
[CBS, earlier tranche](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/epstein-files-released-documents-2025/),
[overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_files))

### Why this is the strong claim

The mention counts require interpretation and invite dispute. This does not.

A statute passed 427–1 required the Justice Department to release the files. The
Justice Department, run by the president's appointees, withheld interview records
in which a woman accused **the president** of abusing her as a minor. The
Department then conceded it may have withheld them wrongly.

Whether the underlying allegation is true is not established and may never be.
What is established is that the people who report to the accused decided the
public would not see the accusation — and were caught doing it.

## 5. Who runs the Justice Department that handles the files

**The claim that Epstein's lawyer leads Trump's DOJ is not accurate, and the
accurate version is arguably worse.**

**Todd Blanche** — Deputy Attorney General, later acting and then Attorney
General — was never Epstein's lawyer. He was **Trump's** criminal defense
lawyer, representing him personally against Manhattan District Attorney Alvin
Bragg and Special Counsel Jack Smith.
([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/trumps-criminal-defense-lawyer-acting-ag-todd-blanche/story?id=131662076),
[NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/todd-blanche-interim-attorney-general-pam-bondi-firing-what-know-rcna266451),
[CNN profile](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/03/politics/todd-blanche-profile-interim-attorney-general))

So the structure is not "Epstein's lawyer runs the DOJ." It is:

> **The president's personal criminal defense attorney was placed in charge of
> the Justice Department, and personally put in charge of releasing the
> investigative files that concern the president.**

This is established by testimony rather than inference. Attorney General Pam
Bondi told lawmakers, in a transcript released later, that Blanche "was in charge
of the process and the entire release of the Epstein files."
([CNN, on the Bondi transcript](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/04/politics/pam-bondi-epstein-testimony-todd-blanche-transcript),
[Public Citizen's timeline](https://www.citizen.org/article/todd-blanche-the-epstein-files-a-timeline/),

The withholding documented in section 4 happened under that arrangement.

### The one who really was on the other side: Alexander Acosta

a week.**

## 6. Maxwell: the interview, the transfer, and the rule that was waived

### The sequence

**July 2025** — Deputy Attorney General **Todd Blanche personally interviewed
Ghislaine Maxwell for roughly nine hours over two days.** This was the first time
the Justice Department had approached her legal team in this way; Bondi directed
it and chose Blanche to do it.

**In that interview, Maxwell told Blanche she had never witnessed Trump doing
anything inappropriate.**

**One week later**, the Bureau of Prisons moved her from **FCI Tallahassee**, a
low-security federal correctional institution, to **FPC Bryan** in Texas — a
minimum-security prison camp.

([NBC News, on the meeting](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/deputy-attorney-general-meet-epstein-accomplice-ghislaine-maxwell-rcna219652),
[NBC, second day](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/live-blog/rcna219895?page=4),
[Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/amid-furor-over-epstein-ties-175000396.html),
[NPR, on FPC Bryan](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/03/nx-s1-5519224/ghislaine-maxwell-prison-texas-epstein),
[Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/ghislaine-maxwell-quietly-moved-minimum-security-prison-what-we-know-2107673),
[inside the facility](https://www.newsweek.com/inside-ghislaine-maxwell-texas-minimum-security-prison-2107984),
[Federal Prison Camp, Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Prison_Camp,_Bryan))

### The rule this broke

**Maxwell's conviction disqualified her from that placement under written Bureau
of Prisons policy.**

Bureau of Prisons policy assigns a **Public Safety Factor** to inmates with sex
offense histories. The effect of that designation is explicit: such inmates

> "are not appropriate for placement at an institution which would permit inmate
> access to the community (i.e., MINIMUM security)."

A minimum-security camp is exactly an institution permitting community access.
Maxwell was convicted of sex trafficking a minor.

The PSF can be **waived** — but only on review and approval by the administrator
of the Designation and Sentence Computation Center. It is an exception requiring
a documented justification, not a routine transfer.

Former BOP officials called the move **"highly unusual."**
([Sen. Whitehouse's demand for the documents, which lays out the policy](https://www.whitehouse.senate.gov/news/release/whitehouse-demands-documents-on-transfer-of-ghislaine-maxwell-to-minimum-security-bureau-of-prisons-facility/),
[Fox News, on former officials' reaction](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ghislaine-maxwell-prison-transfer-called-highly-unusual-former-federal-prison-official),
[criminal defense analysis of the policy](https://www.nealdavislaw.com/blog/sex-crimes/ghislaine-maxwell-texas-minimum-security-prison/),
[further analysis](https://www.danielstark.com/blog/ghislaine-maxwell-transferred-to-minimum-security-prison-in-bryan-texas/),
[why the transfer drew scrutiny](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/why-ghislaine-maxwells-transfer-minimum-190716173.html))

### What the paperwork shows — and does not

The transfer form was released in **August 2026** after sustained congressional
pressure. It confirms that a process occurred and names **Rick Stover, special
assistant to the director**, as the approving official.

**The justification for waiving the Public Safety Factor is redacted.**

([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-31/ghislaine-maxwell-s-prison-transfer-form-released),

### Then they changed the rule

This is the part that outlasts Maxwell.

On **May 6, 2026**, under Blanche's supervision while he was acting Attorney
General, the Bureau of Prisons policy was revised to give **the Attorney General
personal authority to designate or redesignate where any federal prisoner serves
their sentence**, overriding the agency's classification guidelines.

A determination that had belonged to career BOP officials applying written
criteria now rests with a single political appointee.

**Liz Oyer**, the former DOJ pardon attorney who first disclosed the change —
and who was fired after refusing to recommend restoring Mel Gibson's gun rights
— called it "absolutely stunning in scope and potential impact."

([The Marshall Project](https://www.themarshallproject.org/2026/09/18/blanche-changes-federal-prison-placements),
[The Bulwark](https://www.thebulwark.com/p/todd-blanche-ghislaine-maxwell-bureau-of-prisons-policy),
[Raw Story](https://www.rawstory.com/bureau-of-prisons-todd-blanche/),
[Criminal Justice Journalists](https://crimjj.wordpress.com/2026/09/18/after-maxwell-prison-shift-doj-gave-ag-power-to-assign-inmates/),
[KTAL](https://www.ktalnews.com/news/us-politics/federal-prisoner-transfer-authority/))

The documented sequence:

1. A rule barred a convicted child sex trafficker from minimum-security custody.
2. She was interviewed by the president's former defense lawyer and said nothing
   incriminating about the president.
3. A week later the rule was waived for her, and the justification was redacted.
4. The rule was then rewritten so that the president's former defense lawyer can
   do the same thing for anyone, without a waiver.

Steps 1 through 3 are contested as to motive. **Step 4 is a matter of published
policy**, and its effect is that the discretion exercised in step 3 no longer
requires explanation to anyone.

See also [Pardons and Clemency](pardons-and-clemency.md) — the same discretionary
machinery, applied to release rather than placement.

## 7. The cast, sorted out

A common framing is that "the same players from the Epstein saga are still
around Trump." That is half right, and the half that is wrong matters — because
the accurate version is a cleaner indictment than the loose one.

**Sorting the cast by when they actually enter the story:**

| Person | Role in the Epstein case itself | Role now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alexander Acosta** | **Yes** — U.S. Attorney who signed the 2007 non-prosecution agreement | Trump's Labor Secretary, 2017–2019; resigned |
| **Barry Krischer** | **Yes** — Palm Beach State Attorney who took it to a grand jury on one solicitation count | None |
| **Todd Blanche** | **No** — Trump's personal defense lawyer | Attorney General; ran the file release; interviewed Maxwell |
| **Pam Bondi** | **No** — took office in 2011, after the deal | Attorney General until 2026; directed Blanche's Maxwell interview |
| **Ghislaine Maxwell** | **Yes** — convicted co-conspirator | Seeking commutation; moved to a minimum-security camp |

**So the accurate statement is not that Epstein's people were hired.** It is
this:

> The only genuine Epstein-case figure who joined the administration was the
> prosecutor who let Epstein go — and he was given a cabinet seat. Everyone else
> handling the Epstein matter now came to it from Trump's personal legal defense.

Taken together, the pattern is not a holdover network. It is that both ends of
this case — the original non-prosecution and the present disclosure — have been
handled by people whose prior professional relationship was to the person with
the most to lose.

### The Bondi correction

**Pam Bondi did not botch the Florida prosecution of Epstein.** The claim
circulates widely and is unsupported by the chronology.

The Florida handling belongs to two other people:

- **Barry Krischer**, Palm Beach County State Attorney, who resisted the Palm
  Beach Police Department's push to charge Epstein with multiple felonies, took
  the case to a grand jury, and presented it so narrowly that it returned **a
  single count of solicitation of prostitution**. Police Chief Michael Reiter was
  sufficiently alarmed by Krischer's "highly unusual" conduct that he took the
  case to the FBI. Grand jury transcripts later unsealed show **victims being
  questioned as though they were the offenders** — one was asked whether she knew
  she had "committed a crime."
  ([ABC News on the unsealed grand jury materials](https://abc7news.com/post/grand-jury-docs-2006-epstein-case-palm-beach-county-florida/15018104/),
  [the transcripts](https://news.yahoo.com/news/jeffrey-epstein-secret-documents-just-184451449.html),
  [court filing, PDF](https://medialaw.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/06.29.23epstein.pdf),
  [Palm Beach Post litigation to unseal](https://news.yahoo.com/news/jeffrey-epstein-judge-receives-2006-135808794.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall))

- **Alexander Acosta**, federally — section 5 above.

**Bondi became Florida Attorney General in January 2011**, roughly three years
after the plea was entered. She could not have botched a prosecution that was
already over.

**The criticism that does stick** is different and less dramatic: across **eight
years** as Florida's chief law enforcement officer — 2011 to 2019, while
Epstein's network was still operating and while she made sex trafficking a
