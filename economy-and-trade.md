## 1. Withdrawing from the Trans-Pacific Partnership

The TPP was, whatever else it was, an American-authored framework for writing
the trade rules of the Pacific without China at the table. Withdrawing from it
did not kill the agreement — it killed American participation in it. China
promptly began leading its own regional partnership talks, and the remaining
eleven members concluded the deal without the United States.
([The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2017/jan/23/trump-pull-out-trans-pacific-partnership))

The United States gave up the seat and kept the grievance.

## 2. The China trade war

Trump launched a tariff war against China — and, in a bonus move, against the
EU, Canada, and Mexico — and achieved essentially none of his stated objectives.

A United Nations study of the tariffs' effects:

> "More in general the results of this paper point to the fact that the United
> States tariffs on China are economically hurting both countries. United States
> losses are largely related to the higher prices for consumers, while China's
> losses are related to significant export losses."
>
> — UNCTAD ([PDF](https://unctad.org/en/PublicationsLibrary/ser-rp-2019d9_en.pdf))

The mechanism is the relevant point: **tariffs are paid by American importers
and passed through to American consumers.** The "China is paying us billions"
framing was never accurate.

On the outcome:

> The reality is that the Trump administration achieved almost none of its
> goals; it has basically declared victory while going into headlong retreat.
>
> — [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/12/16/opinion/trump-china-trade.html) (opinion)

## 3. The unemployment claim

Trump — and the people amplifying him — claimed credit for the lowest
unemployment rate "ever."
([MSNBC](https://www.msnbc.com/rachel-maddow-show/sotu-trump-plays-make-believe-wildly-exaggerates-economic-record-n1131356))

Several things are wrong with the boast:

**It is not his trend line.** The Obama administration took unemployment from
roughly 10% to roughly 4% — a six-point improvement. Trump took it from roughly
4% to roughly 3.6% — a 0.4-point improvement, at a *slower* rate of decline than
the trend he inherited.
([Business Insider, charts comparing the Trump, Obama, and Bush economies](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/9-charts-comparing-trump-economy-to-obama-bush-administrations-2019-9-1028833119#wage-growth-for-non-supervisory-and-production-workers-in-the-private-sector-slowed-down-after-the-recession-but-mostly-picked-up-during-president-trump-s-first-term-as-the-labor-market-has-tightened-4))

**The headline rate is a poor measure of prosperity.** It counts anyone with any
paid work as employed and excludes anyone who has stopped looking.
([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/personal-finance/smart-money/low-unemployment-many-discouraged-about-jobs/))
Part of one reported drop — 4.8% to 4.6% — came from roughly 490,000 people
*leaving the labor force* in a single month, which is a warning sign, not an
achievement.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-48145563))

**Underemployment never recovered from the Great Recession**, holding around 8%.
([America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/annual/measure/Underemployed/state/ALL))

> Perhaps the BLS should take the advice from this paper and consider a new
> measure of unemployment: a U-7 figure that looks at underemployment as a
> portion of employment. Otherwise, the Fed's governors may assume the economy
> and labor markets are in strong position and make important decisions based on
> faulty assumptions.
>
> — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/eriksherman/2018/09/25/underemployment-takes-an-outsized-toll-on-the-economy-according-to-a-new-study/#46c20126234e)

**Real wages have barely moved in decades**, contrary to what a genuinely tight
labor market would produce.
([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2018/08/07/for-most-us-workers-real-wages-have-barely-budged-for-decades/))

**The jobs are concentrated in large, low-paying occupations.**
([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2014/04/14/u-s-workforce-more-concentrated-in-large-and-largely-low-paid-occupations/))

**Correction to claims in circulation:** The assertion that "persons on
disability are at an all-time high" is commonly cited to the BLS release
[*Persons with a Disability: Labor Force Characteristics*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/disabl.pdf).
That release measures the *employment* of people with disabilities, not the size
of the disability rolls, and does not support the claim.

What it does establish is that the labor force participation rate for people with
a disability is roughly a third that of people without one — a population largely
invisible to the headline unemployment figure.

## 4. The debt

Trump promised to eliminate the national debt in eight years. It was instead
projected to be at least 50% higher than when he took office.
([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2019/03/12/trump-vowed-eliminate-debt-years-hes-track-leave-it-least-percent-higher))

**Context:** The federal debt rose from roughly $19.9 trillion in January 2017
to roughly $27.8 trillion in January 2021 — a ~40% increase, a significant part
of it from pandemic emergency spending, and a significant part of it from the
2017 tax cut below.

## 5. The 2017 tax cut

- It cost homeowners an estimated $1 trillion in lost home value, chiefly
  through the cap on state and local tax deductions and the mortgage interest
  limits.
  ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/10/10/how-trump-tax-bill-affects-homeowners-middle-class/))

- It was sold as an investment surge. It produced a buyback surge — see below.

- The corresponding budget: Trump presented a budget that both failed basic
  arithmetic scrutiny and broke his explicit promise not to cut Medicare,
  Medicaid, and Social Security. His own party's Senate Budget Committee chairman
  declined to hold a markup on it.
  ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2020/02/10/senate-committee-enzi-hearing-trump-budget-113650))

## 6. The market was not the economy

The argument, laid out in order:

1. **Most of the market's gains came from companies buying their own stock**,
   funded by the $1 trillion tax cut — which left a large budget deficit behind.
   ([Money](https://money.com/companies-spending-trump-tax-cuts-stock-buybacks/))

2. **When that ran out, cheap debt took over.** The Fed's posture — under
   sustained public pressure from Trump — let companies borrow at near-zero real
   cost to buy still more of their own shares.
   ([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/economy/as-it-faces-pressure-from-trump-and-investors-the-fed-prepares-to-take-its-biggest-gamble-in-years/2019/07/28/38edfc52-af22-11e9-a0c9-6d2d7818f3da_story.html))

3. **Buybacks shrink the float, which mechanically inflates earnings per
   share** — without a dollar of additional earnings. Executive compensation is
   frequently tied to EPS.
   ([Investopedia on CEO compensation](https://www.investopedia.com/managing-wealth/guide-ceo-compensation/),
   [Investopedia on EPS](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/e/eps.asp))

4. **So the record profits were substantially an accounting artifact** — the
   same pie cut into fewer slices, reported as a bigger pie.

5. **The liquidity underneath it was thinner than advertised.** By March 2020,
   Goldman Sachs was warning that liquidity was drying up across a wide swath of
   the $15 trillion Treasury market.
   ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/fierce-bond-market-swings-dry-up-liquidity-in-wide-swathe-of-15-trillion-us-bond-market-2020-03-12))

6. **Then a real crisis arrived** — one that could not be tweeted out of
   existence — and the structure did what leveraged structures do.
   ([Fortune, comparing the Obama and Trump market records after the crash](https://fortune.com/2020/03/11/obama-trump-stock-market-us-economy/))

The executives whose compensation was tied to the metrics inflated at step 3
retained that compensation.

## 7. The 2025 bill: H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act

Signed July 4, 2025. The structure is the 2017 tax cut's, repeated with the
financing made explicit.

**What it does:** permanently extends the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act
provisions and adds large business tax cuts — financed in part by cuts to clean
energy incentives, **Medicaid, the Affordable Care Act, and the Supplemental
Nutrition Assistance Program**, and other programs serving low-income
households.
([Brookings / Hutchins Center, preliminary assessment](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/one-big-beautiful-bill-a-preliminary-assessment/))

The Congressional Budget Office's letters to Congress are the primary documents;
CBO scoring projected millions losing health coverage.

**What is worth being precise about:** this is not a tax cut that happens to
coincide with benefit cuts. The benefit cuts are the pay-for. The bill's own
arithmetic routes money from Medicaid, ACA subsidies, and food assistance into
permanent business tax reductions. Anyone defending it has to defend that trade,
not each half separately.

For the parallel 2020-era episode — cutting SNAP for 700,000 people during a
pandemic — see [COVID-19 Response](covid-19-response.md).
