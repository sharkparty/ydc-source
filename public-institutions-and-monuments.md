# Public Institutions and Monuments

What happens to shared public property when it is treated as personal property:
the Kennedy Center, the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, and the National Park
Service.

## 1. The Kennedy Center

**What happened.** Trump ousted the Kennedy Center's leadership, installed a
handpicked board, and that board named him chairman. His name was then added to
the building's façade.

**The court said no.** In May 2026, Judge Christopher Cooper ruled the renaming
illegal:

> "May the John F. Kennedy Center … be renamed absent Congressional
> authorization? The answer … is no."

([ABC7 New York](https://abc7ny.com/post/federal-judge-rules-pres-trumps-name-was-illegally-added-kennedy-center-blocks-closure-planned-renovations/19194715/))

**The board did it again anyway.** On August 13, 2026, the board voted to re-add
his name — as "Restored and Renovated By President Donald J. Trump" — and to
close the center for two years, in defiance of the court order. The center
closed its doors in September 2026 regardless (New York Times, September 15,
2026). Trump had threatened to cancel the $257 million renovation if his name
were not restored.
([AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-kennedy-center-closure-renovations-591cc891a1c0ececf3602ed121ce4840))

**On the word "destroying":** it is a characterization rather than a finding.
The documented facts are that a national cultural institution had its leadership
replaced, had its board name the president as chairman, had its building renamed
in violation of a federal statute, defied the court order holding that renaming
unlawful, and is now closed.

## 2. The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool

A small story that functions as a complete one.

**April 9, 2025** — Trump announces on Truth Social that he is renovating the
Reflecting Pool.
([People](https://people.com/donald-trump-remodel-lincoln-memorial-reflecting-pool-11947418))

**April 23** — At an Oval Office meeting, Trump says work has begun. Crews are
photographed applying a blue material to the emptied basin.
([video](https://youtu.be/ewHUqlCxEJM?si=JdkEjk78B9tTUGsK))

> "I have a guy who's unbelievable at doing swimming pools."

The contract was awarded without bidding, to a Mar-a-Lago neighbor.

**June 4** — Trump announces the work is finished. The pool is refilled by June
9.

**June 10** — One day after refilling, the water turns green. The Interior
Department blames "residual algae from the supply lines."

**Shortly after** — the blue coating begins to peel.

**June 19** — Former U.S. Olympian David Hearn is arrested and detained for five
hours after visiting. The charge is destruction of government property; Hearn
says he touched a piece of the peeling material.

([Fast Company, full timeline](https://www.fastcompany.com/91562873/complete-timeline-trump-reflecting-pool-drama))

A no-bid contract to a personal associate, a pool guy doing work on a national
memorial, a failure visible within 24 hours, and a citizen detained for five
hours for touching the evidence. The scale is trivial. Everything else about it
is not.

## 3. The National Park Service

### Where the money went

Internal NPS records show **$323 million routed to White House grounds
projects** — against $7.5 million in 2024, an increase of roughly 5,000% — plus
about $100 million to the National Mall, while the budgets for Yellowstone,
Yosemite, and the Grand Canyon were cut.
([Scott MacFarlane](https://macfarlanenews.substack.com/p/trump-turns-national-parks-into-piggybank))

The FY2027 request is a **32% cut to the Park Service**, alongside a new **$10
billion "Presidential Capital Stewardship Program"** for projects in Washington,
D.C.
([legis1](https://legis1.com/news/nps-budget-cuts-trump-funding-32-house-rejects))

### The ballroom

Estimated cost: **$400 million.** Congress appropriated less than 1% of it.
*National Trust for Historic Preservation v. National Park Service* produced an
appeals court ruling that construction must stop.
([Constitutional Accountability Center case page](https://www.theusconstitution.org/litigation/national-trust-for-historic-preservation-v-national-park-service/))

### The deaths

An Interior Department memo issued in **December 2025** directs all Interior
bureaus and offices — the Park Service, the Bureau of Land Management, and
others — **not to confirm a death**, or any "fatalities, suspected fatalities,
serious injuries, or emotionally sensitive incidents," and never to "be the
first to inform the public of a fatality or suspected suicide."

This reverses the Park Service's long-standing practice, summarized internally
as "maximum disclosure, minimum delay," under which a death was typically
disclosed within 24 to 48 hours.

Interior press secretary Aubrie Spady told the Washington Post the guidance was
meant to "create a more consistent approach to incident communications across
the Department and is not intended to conceal fatalities or delay information."

([E&E News / Politico](https://www.eenews.net/articles/national-park-service-muzzles-fatality-reports-alarming-rangers/),
[Coalition to Protect America's National Parks](https://protectnps.org/2026/06/25/internal-memo-orders-staff-not-to-reveal-deaths-in-national-parks/),
[Truthout](https://truthout.org/articles/new-trump-admin-policy-hides-deaths-at-national-parks-from-public/),
[Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/outdoor-adventure/environment/nps-internal-memo-deaths/),
[St. George News](https://www.stgeorgeutah.com/life/department-of-the-interior-changes-policy-about-reporting-deaths-injuries-in-national-parks/article_14353bd8-568b-4572-a744-0af9af26c5f3.html))

Until this documentation surfaced, the claim circulated without a traceable
source and was not citable. It is now.
