officials. Every claim carries a citation, and every citation is linked for
independent verification.

## Scope and status

The bulk of the first-term material was compiled between 2016 and mid-2020, with
additions through 2022. The second-term material was compiled in September 2026
and covers events from January 2025 onward.

Figures, projections, and phrases like "as of today" reflect the moment they
were written; where a number was later superseded by a better one, a **Context**
note says so rather than quietly rewriting history. Several 2025–2026 entries
describe litigation and investigations that were still open when this was
written — those say so, and say what had and had not been decided.

Some of the later material was assembled with the assistance of AI research
tools. Such tools return search results and citations intermixed, and
occasionally attach a claim to a source that does not support it. Each source was
checked against the claim it was attached to before use; those supporting no
claim were moved to [the appendix](appendix-unattached-sources.md) rather than
left in place resembling citations.

## Conventions used here

- **Every claim gets a source.** If a claim could not be traced to a source, it
  is marked **Unverified** and left in place rather than deleted or dressed up.
- **Allegation, charge, finding, and conviction are different things**, and the
  entries say which. An open lawsuit, a minority committee report, and a court
  ruling carry different weight, and collapsing them is how a sourced document
  turns into a rumor mill.
- **Context notes** (`**Context:**`) supply background an entry assumes, or record
  where a figure was later superseded.
- **Correction notes** (`**Correction:**`) mark claims in circulation that
  overstate, misattribute, or garble the underlying record. Corrections appear
  beside the claims they address rather than replacing them silently.
- Sources are cited by publication where practical, so a dead link is still a
  findable citation.
- **Congressional letters and minority-staff reports are labeled as such.** They
  are real documents that say what they say. They are not findings, and a file
  that leans on them says so at the top.
- The analysis is openly argumentative; the sourcing is not. The two are
  separable, and the citations stand independently of the conclusions drawn from
  them.

## Contributing

Corrections take priority over additions. An entry shown to be wrong by a cited
source will be corrected or struck. Entries missing sources should be reported;
an unsourced claim in this repository is a liability rather than an asset.
