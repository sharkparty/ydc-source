## 2. Veterans, POWs, and Gold Star families

### John McCain

> "He's not a war hero. He's a war hero because he was captured. … I like people
> who weren't captured."
> ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/story/2015/07/trump-attacks-mccain-i-like-people-who-werent-captured-120317),
> [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/opinion/2019/06/04/dday-anniversary-trump-disrespects-mccain-prisoners-of-war-column/1327852001/))

Said by a man who received five draft deferments — several for education, and
one for bone spurs, documented by a family doctor.

General James Mattis, retired, on the subject:

> "I got my spurs on the battlefield. Trump got his from a doctor's note."

### The Khan family

Khizr and Ghazala Khan, whose son Army Captain Humayun Khan was killed in Iraq,
criticized Trump at the 2016 Democratic convention. Trump attacked them, then
escalated over several days, ignoring his own advisers and Republican Party
leaders who asked him to stop. He told a Virginia television station he had no
regrets.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/08/03/us/politics/donald-trump-gop.html))

## 5. Lying as a default mode

**The volume.** In his first ten months in office, Trump told nearly six times
as many falsehoods as Obama did across his entire eight-year presidency.
([The New York Times lie count](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2017/12/14/opinion/sunday/trump-lies-obama-who-is-worse.html))

Because the obvious objection is methodology — the standard applied was
identical to both men, and the collection method is documented:
([methodology discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/dataisbeautiful/comments/7jt7ke/in_his_first_10_months_trump_told_nearly_six/dr9hsvh))

**The running totals**, maintained independently by three outlets:

- [The Washington Post Fact Checker database](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2019/12/16/president-trump-has-made-false-or-misleading-claims-over-days/)
- [PolitiFact's file on Trump](https://www.politifact.com/personalities/donald-trump/)
- [The Toronto Star's fact check project](https://projects.thestar.com/donald-trump-fact-check/)

The frequently repeated claim that no recording of him speaking exists without a
falsehood in it is rhetoric rather than a finding. The documented figure is
substantial on its own: the *Post* logged more than 30,000 false or misleading
claims across the term, at a rate that accelerated each year.

## 6. The speech itself

Two verbatim examples, offered without editing, of extended public remarks. The
original compiler filed these under "signs of dementia." That is a diagnosis
neither of us is qualified to make; what can be said without qualification is
that these are the words of a sitting president, transcribed exactly.

**On nuclear weapons:**

> "Look, having nuclear — my uncle was a great professor and scientist and
> engineer, Dr. John Trump at MIT; good genes, very good genes, OK, very smart,
> the Wharton School of Finance, very good, very smart — you know, if you're a
> conservative Republican, if I were a liberal, if, like, OK, if I ran as a
> liberal Democrat, they would say I'm one of the smartest people anywhere in
> the world — it's true! — but when you're a conservative Republican they try —
> oh, do they do a number — that's why I always start off: Went to Wharton, was
> a good student, went there, went there, did this, built a fortune — you know I
> have to give my like credentials all the time, because we're a little
> disadvantaged — but you look at the nuclear deal, the thing that really
> bothers me — it would have been so easy, and it's not as important as these
> lives are (nuclear is powerful; my uncle explained that to me many, many years
> ago, the power and that was 35 years ago; he would explain the power of what's
> going to happen and he was right — who would have thought?), but when you look
> at what's going on with the four prisoners — now it used to be three, now it's
> four — but when it was three and even now, I would have said it's all in the

> know, it's gonna take them about another 150 years — but the Persians are

**On wind power:**

## 7. The presidency, conducted on Twitter

- **Insults as a primary output.** A *New York Times* review found that
  approximately **one in every eight** of Trump's tweets was a personal insult
  of some kind.
  ([The New York Times review, catalogued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_on_social_media#Insults))

- **Moving markets by accident.** Impulsive tweets repeatedly moved the stock
  market, at times erasing hundreds of billions in value in an afternoon.
  ([Barron's](https://www.barrons.com/articles/donald-trump-twitter-stock-market-51567803655))

- **Firing cabinet officials by tweet.** Secretary of State Rex Tillerson
  learned he had been fired from a tweet.
  ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2018/3/13/17113950/trump-state-department-rex-tillerson-fired-tweet-twitter))

  National Security Adviser John Bolton's departure was announced the same way,
  on September 10, 2019: Trump tweeted that Bolton's "services are no longer
  needed," and Bolton replied within minutes that he had offered to resign the
  previous night. Both men learned their status as the public did.
  ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2019/09/10/bolton-trump-fires-national-security-advisor-via-twitter/),
  [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2019/09/10/john-bolton-fired-donald-trump-tweet))

- **Promoting anti-vaccine conspiracy theories**, in his own words, before he
  ran.
  ([@realDonaldTrump, 2014](https://twitter.com/realDonaldTrump/status/449525268529815552?s=19))

## 8. Contempt for the free press

- **Asking the FBI Director to consider imprisoning journalists.** James Comey's
  account has Trump suggesting reporters who publish classified material should
  go to prison.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/05/16/trump-allegedly-asked-comey-to-consider-imprisoning-members-of-the-press.html))

- **"Fake news" as a term of art.** Trump did not coin the phrase, but he
  successfully inverted it: from "fabricated stories presented as journalism" to
  "journalism I dislike."
  ([Usage of the term by Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news#Usage_of_the_term_by_Donald_Trump))

- **Promoting actual fabrications** from the *National Enquirer*, Alex Jones,
  Fox News opinion segments, Russian state media, OAN, and anonymous accounts —
  including manufactured scandals amplified from the presidential account.
  ([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2019/09/25/trump-is-pushing-fake-scandal-once-again-dont-buy-it/))

## 9. Attacks on American institutions

Sustained public attacks on the free press, Congress, the FBI, the CIA, and the
opposition party — not as policy criticism but as delegitimization.

- Demanding that four sitting congresswomen apologize to him and to the United
  States, after telling them to "go back" to the countries they came from (three
  of the four were born in the U.S.).
  ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-demands-radical-left-congresswomen-apologize-him-u-s-israel-n1029831))
- Publicly disparaging the intelligence community's assessments and telling its
  leaders to "go back to school."
  ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/01/30/us/politics/trump-intelligence.html))
- An unprecedented study tracking thousands of attacks on individuals and
  institutions from the presidential platform.
  ([The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2019/nov/21/inside-the-beltway-unprecedented-study-tracks-2235/))

## 10. Encouraging violence

Trump repeatedly encouraged or excused violence at his rallies, offered to pay
the legal fees of supporters who assaulted protesters, and stayed silent when
his rhetoric was cited by people who acted on it — including a Coast Guard
lieutenant who compiled a target list of journalists and Democratic politicians.
