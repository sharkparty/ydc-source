## 1. The inheritance

Trump received the equivalent of at least $413 million from his father's real
estate empire, much of it through tax schemes that *The New York Times* found
included instances of outright fraud — chiefly a sham purchasing company used to
disguise gifts as business expenses, and undervalued property transfers.
([The New York Times investigation](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2018/10/02/us/politics/donald-trump-tax-schemes-fred-trump.html))

## 3. Trump University

A for-profit "university" that was neither a university nor an education — a
high-pressure sales operation that took students for thousands of dollars each
on the promise of Trump's personally selected instructors and secrets.

- New York Attorney General Eric Schneiderman sued Trump **personally**, and a
  judge had already found him personally liable for operating the enterprise
  without the license New York requires.

- He settled the fraud suits for **$25 million**, covering more than 6,000
  victims, in the weeks between the election and the inauguration — after years
  of insisting he would never settle.
  ([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/source-trump-nearing-settlement-in-trump-university-fraud-cases/2016/11/18/8dc047c0-ada0-11e6-a31b-4b6397e625d0_story.html),
  [settlement details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_University#Settlement),
  [Xinhua](http://www.xinhuanet.com//english/2016-11/19/c_135842360.htm#1))

## 4. The Trump Foundation

Trump ran a fraudulent charity and was permanently barred from leading any other
charity in New York State, with his children placed under court-ordered
training. Among the documented misuses: $264,231 of charitable money redirected
to repair a fountain outside one of his hotels.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/12/10/nyregion/trump-foundation-lawsuit-attorney-general.html),
[full list of legal and ethical findings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_J._Trump_Foundation#Legal_and_ethical_controversies))

He also admitted to using the foundation's money to fund his own campaign, which
is illegal.

The court's own characterization was **"a shocking pattern of illegality"**:
charitable funds used to settle Trump's personal lawsuits, to buy a portrait of
himself, and to support the 2016 campaign in coordination with campaign staff.
$2 million in damages was ordered on top of the distribution of remaining
assets.

He had publicly pledged more than **$8.5 million** to charity over fifteen years
and delivered roughly a third of it.
([Mother Jones, on the longer pattern](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2025/10/trump-230-million-justice-department-settlement-charity-grift/?utm_source=mj-newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily-newsletter-10-23-2025))

Case status and outcome: [Legal Record](legal-record.md).

the 1980s and 1990s.

## 7. Mob money and Russian money

Trump's businesses were supported by a network of attorneys and partners linked
to New York organized crime and to Russian oligarchs known to launder money
through real estate — a structural feature of high-end condo sales, and one the
Trump Organization leaned on heavily when conventional lenders would no longer
touch him.

- ([Politico Magazine, on the mob ties](https://www.politico.com/magazine/story/2016/05/donald-trump-2016-mob-organized-crime-213910))
- ([Rep. Eric Swalwell's compiled list of Russia ties](https://swalwell.house.gov/issues/russia-trump-his-administration-s-ties))
- ([Foreign Policy, "How Russian Money Helped Save Trump's Business"](https://foreignpolicy.com/2018/12/21/how-russian-money-helped-save-trumps-business/))

Donald Trump Jr. said the quiet part out loud in 2008, after the Trump
Organization had recovered:

> "Russians make up a pretty disproportionate cross-section of a lot of our
> assets."

## 8. The Baku deal and Iran's Revolutionary Guard

Journalist Adam Davidson's reporting — published as "Donald Trump's Worst Deal"
in *The New Yorker* in March 2017, and summarized by him in a later Twitter
thread — laid out the Trump Organization's involvement in the Trump Tower Baku
project in Azerbaijan:

- The Trump Organization licensed its name to, and was operationally involved
  in, a project widely reported at the time to be a money-laundering vehicle
  connected to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.
- The IRGC branch involved was the one tasked with procuring components,
  including missile guidance technology, for Iran's ballistic missile program.
- The project's Azerbaijani partners — the family of transport minister Ziya
  Mammadov, described in leaked U.S. diplomatic cables as "notoriously corrupt,
  even for Azerbaijan" — spent heavily across the United States looking for an
  American partner. The Trump Organization was the only one that signed.
- The Trump Organization did not meaningfully dispute the underlying facts; it
  disputed the characterization of its role as more than branding, which
  Davidson's reporting contradicts.
- The relationship ran through the 2016 campaign. The Trump Organization cut
  ties with the project roughly a month after the election.

([NPR interview with Adam Davidson on the reporting](https://www.npr.org/2017/03/07/519063946/the-new-yorker-uncovers-trump-hotels-ties-to-corrupt-oligarch-family),
[Davidson's own summary thread](https://twitter.com/adamdavidson/status/1213874465651724290))

**Correction to claims in circulation:** Accounts of this deal frequently
misname the Iranian official as "Mohamed Ghalibaf" and add "the Darvishi
brothers" to the chain; the official named in the reporting is Mohammad Bagher
Ghalibaf. The Trump Organization's counterparty was not Ziya Mammadov directly
but the family business run by his son, Anar Mammadov.

Accounts also frequently assert that Trump "knowingly" laundered money for the
IRGC. The reporting does not establish knowledge. It establishes that the Trump
Organization failed to conduct due diligence that would have surfaced these ties,
in a deal where the warning signs were already public.

## 9. Trump Tower Moscow

Trump pursued a Trump Tower Moscow deal during the 2016 campaign — signing a
letter of intent while running for president — and publicly denied having any
business dealings with Russia the entire time. Michael Cohen went to prison in
part for lying to Congress about the timeline.
([Trump Tower Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_Tower_Moscow))

## 11. The campaign as a brand exercise

Trump ran for president three times before winning — 2000, 2012 (exploratory),
2016 — in a pattern consistent with a marketing strategy for the Trump name
rather than a political project.
([TV Guide's timeline of the campaigns](https://www.tvguide.com/news/donald-trump-presidential-campaign-timeline/))

**Unsourced:** the further claim that Trump did not want to win, inferred from
his reaction on election night. No recording is cited for it in this repository,
and it remains an interpretation rather than a documented finding.
