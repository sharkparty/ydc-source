# COVID-19 Response

Compiled contemporaneously between February and March 2020, then extended. This
is the densest file in the repository because the failure was dense: it happened
in public, on the record, day by day, while the alternative was available the
entire time.

**A note on hindsight:** Almost nothing below relies on knowing how 2020 ended.
The criticisms were made at the time, by named experts, on the record, and the
president's own statements are quoted from White House transcripts. Where a 2020
number was later superseded, a **Context** note says so.

## 3. How bad it actually was, as understood at the time

The comparison that mattered in early 2020 was to seasonal flu and to the 2009
H1N1 pandemic, because those were the analogies being used to dismiss it.

**Lethality.** WHO's March 3, 2020 briefing put the case fatality rate among
reported cases at 3.4%, against roughly 0.1% for seasonal flu.
([WHO Director-General's remarks, March 3, 2020](https://www.who.int/dg/speeches/detail/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-media-briefing-on-covid-19---3-march-2020))
The 2009 H1N1 pandemic's fatality rate was around 0.02%.
([CIDRAP, on the global 2009 H1N1 infection rate](http://www.cidrap.umn.edu/news-perspective/2013/01/study-puts-global-2009-pandemic-h1n1-infection-rate-24))

**Correction to claims in circulation:** The 3.4% figure is frequently
attributed to [PubMed 19545404](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/19545404),
which is a study of H1N1 transmissibility rather than COVID-19 lethality. The
figure originates in the WHO briefing cited above.

**Context:** The 3.4% was a *case* fatality rate among confirmed cases, not an
infection fatality rate. Later estimates of the true IFR clustered around 0.5–1%,
roughly 5–10 times seasonal influenza — a lower figure than the contemporaneous
headline, and still a severe one.

**Transmissibility.** R₀ is the average number of new infections caused by one
infected person in a population with no prior immunity. If R₀ is 3, each case
produces three more.

| Disease | R₀ |
| --- | --- |
| Seasonal flu | ~1.3 ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/19545404)) |
| 2009 H1N1 | 1.4–1.6 ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/19545404)) |
| COVID-19 (early estimates) | 2.2–3.4 ([University of Michigan Health](https://healthblog.uofmhealth.org/wellness-prevention/updated-information-on-covid-19-what-can-you-do-to-protect-against-coronavirus)) |
| COVID-19 (some studies) | 4.7–6.6 ([medRxiv preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.02.07.20021154v1)) |

More lethal *and* more contagious. That combination is why "it's just the flu"
was not a close call.

## 4. The testing failure

**February 5** — The CDC began shipping test kits to state and local labs. The
kits had a technical flaw and did not produce reliable results.

Three things the administration could have done and did not:

1. **Use the working WHO test.** It existed. It was not adopted.
   ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2020/03/06/coronavirus-testing-failure-123166))
2. **Waive the regulations blocking private hospitals and labs from developing
   their own tests.** Those waivers came weeks late.
   ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/03/12/opinion/americans-tested-for-coronavirus.html))
3. **Blame the right people.** Trump instead blamed an Obama-era regulation for
   slowing test production. There was no truth to the claim.
   ([White House transcript of the remarks](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-vice-president-pence-coronavirus-briefing-airline-ceos/))

The result was that the United States fell behind South Korea, Singapore, and
China in a race where the only currency is time.

> "We just twiddled our thumbs as the coronavirus waltzed in."
>
> — William Hanage, Harvard epidemiologist, in
> [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/outlook/coronavirus-testing-united-states/2020/03/05/a6ced5aa-5f0f-11ea-9055-5fa12981bbbf_story.html)

## 5. Who he put in charge

### Mike Pence — February 26

Trump placed Vice President Mike Pence in charge of the federal coronavirus
response. Pence's pitch was his own record as governor during an infectious
disease emergency:

> "As a former governor from the state where the first MERS case emerged in
> 2014, I know full well the importance of presidential leadership, the
> importance of administration leadership, and the vital role of partnerships of
> state and local governments, and health authorities in responding to potential
> threats and dangers infectious diseases."

Asked about that record, Trump said: "I think he's done a phenomenal job on
healthcare. One of the best, if not the best, in the country."
([Politico Magazine, "How Mike Pence Made Indiana's HIV Outbreak Worse"](https://www.politico.com/news/magazine/2020/03/02/how-mike-pence-made-indianas-hiv-outbreak-worse-118648))

What actually happened in Indiana:

- Scott County, in rural southeastern Indiana, saw a spike in HIV cases in 2014
  and 2015. State officials identified the cause — injection drug use — and
  identified who needed testing.
- The intervention that works is a needle exchange. Pence delayed authorizing
  one for months because he needed thirty days to pray about it. Cases grew
  exponentially in the interval.
  ([PolitiFact's assessment of Pence's own account](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2020/mar/05/mike-pence/fact-checking-pences-defense-response-indiana-hiv-/))
- Pence also claimed needle exchanges increase drug use. The CDC says
  otherwise — syringe services programs are associated with people entering
  treatment, not with more injection.
  ([CDC FAQ on syringe services programs](https://www.cdc.gov/ssp/syringe-services-programs-faq.html))
- The outbreak was ultimately contained when Pence took the advice that
  contradicted his own instinct.
- Pence's revisionist retelling, delivered on Hannity:
  [Fox News video](https://video.foxnews.com/v/6136713991001#sp=show-clips)

The pattern being hired for: an ideological instinct, applied against expert
consensus, reversed only after the damage compounds.

## 6. Refusing to order supplies

Trump declined to use the Defense Production Act to compel manufacture of
respirators, masks, and ventilators, because American business asked him not to.

> The U.S. Chamber of Commerce and the heads of major corporations have lobbied
> the administration against using the act. They say the move could prove
> counterproductive, imposing red tape on companies precisely when they need
> flexibility to deal with closed borders and shuttered factories.

Trump, Larry Kudlow, and Jared Kushner all reportedly found this persuasive.
([New York Magazine](https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2020/03/defense-production-act-coronavirus-trump-respirators-masks-ventilators.html))

### No national coordination

In place of a federal procurement strategy, states were told to buy their own
supplies — and then found themselves bidding against each other, and against the
federal government, for the same masks and ventilators. Prices rose accordingly.
([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-03-19/trump-told-governors-to-buy-own-virus-supplies-then-outbid-them))

### Refusing to mobilize the military

Governor Andrew Cuomo of New York published an open letter asking Trump to
deploy the Army Corps of Engineers to build hospital capacity, on the grounds
that time was running out and the options were narrowing.
([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/03/15/opinion/andrew-cuomo-coronavirus-trump.html))

**Note:** Cuomo was governor of New York State, not of New York City, an error
common in secondary accounts.

## 8. Misinformation from the podium

A running list of specific false claims, each with the statement and the
correction.

### "Anybody that wants a test can get a test"

> "Anybody that wants a test can get a test. That's what the bottom line is."
> — March 6, CDC Atlanta
> ([White House transcript](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-tour-centers-disease-control-prevention-atlanta-ga/))

In reality Americans needed a doctor's authorization to be tested, and many
people who had one still could not get access.

### Banning trade with Europe

In the March 11 Oval Office address, reading from a script, Trump said the new
European travel restrictions "will not only apply to the tremendous amount of
trade and cargo, but various other things as we get approval. Anything coming
from Europe to the United States is what we are discussing."

He was not banning trade or cargo, as he was
[forced to clarify on Twitter](https://twitter.com/realDonaldTrump/status/1237924658185469954?s=)
after the address. Markets had already reacted.

### "All travel from Europe"

> "To keep new cases from entering our shores, we will be suspending all travel
> from Europe to the United States for the next 30 days… There will be
> exemptions for Americans who have undergone appropriate screenings… These
> restrictions will also not apply to the United Kingdom." — March 11

It was not all travel from Europe; it applied to the 26 countries of the
Schengen Area. The UK and Ireland — not in Schengen — were added later.
([CNN fact check](https://www.cnn.com/2020/03/12/politics/fact-check-trump-coronavirus-europe-travel/index.html))

### Everyone returning from Europe is tested

> "Sure. But we have them very heavily tested. If an American is coming back or
> anybody is coming back, we're testing. We have a tremendous testing set up
> where people coming in have to be tested." — March 12
> ([White House transcript](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-prime-minister-varadkar-ireland-bilateral-meeting-3/?utm_source=link))

Untrue. Returning travelers were not required to be tested, and no system was
being built to test them.

### "The borders are automatically shut down"

> "In a certain way, you could say that the borders are automatically shut down,
> without having to say 'shut down.'… And we have closed down certain sections
> of the world, frankly, and they've sort of automatically closed them also."
> — March 4
> ([White House transcript](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-vice-president-pence-coronavirus-briefing-airline-ceos/))

Never explained. The restrictions then in place — on China and Iran — exempted
American citizens, permanent residents, and some family members, and were not
border closures.
([CNN fact check](https://www.cnn.com/2020/03/11/politics/fact-check-trump-administration-coronavirus-28-dishonest/index.html))

### "I didn't shake any hands in India"

Asked on March 12 whether he was comfortable shaking hands with visiting heads
of government, Trump said he had not shaken hands on his recent India trip. An
Indian outlet published a photo gallery of seven handshakes from that trip
within hours.
([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/in-pictures/donald-trump-said-he-did-not-shake-hands-with-anyone-in-india-thats-not-true/380650/))

### "Tremendous control"

> "This is a very contagious — this is a very contagious virus. It's incredible.
> But it's something that we have tremendous control of." — March 15 press
> conference
> ([White House transcript](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-vice-president-pence-members-coronavirus-task-force-press-briefing-2/))

At the same briefing, after Trump left the room, Dr. Anthony Fauci said:

> "The worst is, yes, ahead for us. It is how we respond to that challenge
> that's going to determine what the ultimate end point is going to be. We have
> a very, very critical point now."

### Obama and swine flu

> "If you go back and look at the swine flu and what happened with the swine
> flu, you'll see how many people died and how actually nothing was done for
> such a long period of time, as people were dying all over the place."
> ([White House transcript](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-vice-president-pence-members-coronavirus-task-force-press-conference-3/))

What actually happened:

- **April 26, 2009** — Less than two weeks after the first confirmed U.S. H1N1
  cases, the Obama administration declared a public health emergency.
  ([Texas DSHS record](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/idcu/disease/influenza/surveillance/2010/))
- **April 28, 2009** — An initial $1.5 billion funding request to Congress
  ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-flu-usa-cases-sb/obama-seeks-1-5-billion-for-swine-flu-as-cases-jump-idUSWAT01136620090429));
  Congress ultimately appropriated $7.7 billion
  ([CIDRAP](http://www.cidrap.umn.edu/news-perspective/2009/06/congress-approves-765-billion-pandemic-flu-response)).
  The same day, a CDC test was cleared for use.
- **May 1, 2009** — CDC began shipping tests across the U.S. and worldwide.
  Between April 23 and May 31 the CDC's influenza laboratory analyzed about
  5,000 samples.
- **October 2009** — Obama declared a national emergency to give hospitals
  flexibility for a possible surge.
  ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/10/25/us/politics/25flu.html))

Side by side:

| | H1N1 (2009) | COVID-19 (2020) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public health emergency declared | April 2009, within 2 weeks of first U.S. cases | January 31, 2020 |
| WHO declares pandemic | June 2009 | March 11, 2020 |
| National emergency declared | October 2009 | March 13, 2020 |
| Working test shipped | May 1, 2009 | Flawed kits Feb 5; functional capacity took weeks longer |

([H1N1 timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_flu_pandemic_in_the_United_States#History))

H1N1 infected roughly 57 million Americans with a fatality rate of 0.01–0.08% —
about 12,000 U.S. deaths.

**Correction to claims in circulation:** Contemporaneous projections extrapolated
3.42 million American deaths from 57 million infections at a 6% fatality rate. No
credible estimate supported 6%. At the ~1% infection fatality rate later
established, 57 million infections implies roughly 570,000 deaths.

**Context:** Recorded U.S. COVID-19 deaths passed one million by May 2022.

### Flu deaths

> from the flu?' And, in this country, I think last year was 36- or 37,000

> ([White House transcript](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-members-coronavirus-task-force-meeting-pharmaceutical-companies/))

He may not have known it. The CDC

> ([transcript](https://factba.se/transcript/donald-trump-interview-sean-hannity-fox-telephone-march-4-2020))

The 1989–90 flu season was bad enough that the CDC
[declared an epidemic](https://www.nytimes.com/1990/01/26/us/us-health-agency-declares-flu-epidemic.html).
A 2010 CDC analysis
[estimated](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5933a1.htm)
26,582 deaths — about a quarter of what he claimed.

### "The Democrats have no plan"

They had published one.
([Office of the Speaker, March 11, 2020](https://www.speaker.gov/newsroom/31120-2)) It called for:

- Free coronavirus testing for everyone who needs a test, including the
  uninsured;
- Paid emergency leave — 14 days of paid sick leave and up to three months of
  paid family and medical leave;
- Enhanced unemployment insurance, extending protections to furloughed workers;
- Strengthened food security: SNAP, student meals, senior nutrition, food banks;
- Protections for frontline workers, including health care and cleaning staff in
  at-risk settings;
- Increased federal Medicaid funds as state costs rose.

Most of it became law within two weeks.

The administration's own plan, as of March 12, 2020 — the day before the
emergency declaration, seven weeks after the first U.S. case — was not
published, because there was not one. Americans asking what they were supposed
to do had no federal answer to consult.

### Breaking the government's own crisis-communication rules

During the George W. Bush administration the federal government developed
guidelines for communicating in a public health crisis. The core principles are
**be first, be right, be credible, show respect, promote action**.
([CDC Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication manual, PDF](https://emergency.cdc.gov/cerc/ppt/cerc_2014edition_Copy.pdf))

*The Washington Post* found the administration's response was "breaking almost
every rule in the book."
([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2020/03/14/cdc-manual-crisis-coronavirus-trump/))

The practical cost of inconsistent and incorrect information from the White
House was that Americans did not know what they were supposed to do.

## 12. What he did to people's safety net during it

  ([Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/news/2020/03/trump-administration-food-stamp-cuts-coronavirus))

  ([Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2020-03-30/trump-coronavirus-bailout-oversight))
